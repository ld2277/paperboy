## STANDING CORRECTIONS - carry forever
- **AN INDEX OWNER'S OWN PAGE CAN BE STALE AND SELF-CONTRADICTORY.** Nasdaq's SOX *History* page served a stale net change and repeated the current close as the previous close; the *Breakdown* page had the real prior close. **DERIVE THE PERCENT FROM THE PRIOR CLOSE, NEVER FROM A DISPLAYED NET CHANGE, AND CHECK THE TWO AGREE BEFORE PRINTING EITHER.**
- **A SUPERLATIVE MUST NAME ITS BENCHMARK OR IT BREEDS A FOUR-WAY CONFLICT.** Brent -16% "since the start of the pandemic" and WTI -13% "since 2022" are both correct; "oil fell X%" made them look irreconcilable. **"RECORD" AND "WORST EVER" EACH NEED THEIR OWN CHECK - AMONG WHAT, OVER WHAT PERIOD - AND SO DOES THE *INDEX* A DAY-SUPERLATIVE BELONGS TO.**
- **A LIVE BLOG CAN SERVE YESTERDAY'S COPY UNDER TODAY'S DATELINE.** Yahoo's 4:02pm ET entry said chips "ended negative" on a day the index never traded below the prior close. **A LIVE BLOG FETCHED TODAY MAY ALSO SERVE A CACHE HOURS OR MONTHS OLD; A QUOTE PAGE CAN SERVE LAST YEAR'S ROWS. CHECK THE RELEASE INDEX, NOT THE DOCUMENT.**
- **A PAGE'S FURNITURE CAN CONTRADICT ITS OWN BODY, AND THE BODY WINS.** An Al Jazeera photo caption said Pena resigned while the body said he was impeached; a Kyiv Independent headline said 37 injured while its own breakdown summed to 38; Rio Times' metadata said "44 to 1"; an investing.com live ticker contradicted its article text twice on one page. **A TRACKER THAT CONTRADICTS ITSELF IS NOT A SOURCE FOR THE FIGURE IT DISAGREES ABOUT.**
- **A HEADLINE'S LEGAL WORD IS LOOSER THAN THE BODY'S.** "Under arrest" was house arrest; a headline quoting characterising language is not a holding; a TRO is not a merits ruling and a brief order is not an opinion; an inquiry is not a contempt finding; **A CONDITIONAL APPROVAL OR "CONDITIONAL COMMITMENT" IS NOT A LOAN; AN OFFER IS NOT AN AWARD; AN LOI IS NOT AN AWARD; A MOTION IS NOT A WIN; A RENEWED MOTION IMPLIES AN EARLIER REFUSAL - GO FIND IT; A RECOMMENDATION IS NOT AN ADDITION; A TRANSMITTAL DEADLINE IS NOT A CANVASS.**
- **COUNT THE PRESENT, NOT THE CHAMBER.** "44 of 45 with one abstention" implies a present abstainer; it was 44 present, one absent. **"78-0" WAS 78 OF THOSE PRESENT IN AN 80-SEAT CHAMBER. WHEN A VOTE TOTAL AND A CHAMBER SIZE DISAGREE BY ONE, THAT IS THE QUESTION, NOT A ROUNDING.**

## STANDING CORRECTIONS - carry forever
- **A condition spoken before a data release is a tripwire, not a reaction.** Check the speech's clock against the release's clock before writing "after the print".
- **A quote clipped of its venue or date clause is a different claim.** "Should be considered" and "should be considered at the December meeting" commit to different things.
- **A speech index that lists no speech is evidence there was none.** Two of a secondary's quotes belonged to a 2024 conference and an October wire; the Dallas Fed's own page settled it in one fetch.
- **A politician's insult is a factual claim and verifies like one.** "Defeated former Congressman" was false as to "former" and six months stale as to "defeated".
- **A board passes capital appropriations, not a spending budget**, and "up to" is a cap on an authorisation, never money spent. Write "authorised up to".
- **A 10% round-up is an error, not a rounding.** $900.9bn is not "close to $1 trillion".
- **Two renderings of one wire at different timestamps are two intraday snapshots, not two sources** - and only the later one can possibly be a close. Neither establishes one without a time zone.
- **A referral to committee is not a motion to table**, even when the practical effect is identical. Get the chamber's question, not the headline's verb.
- **A suspicion is a reason to check, not to cut.** The Brent settlement that looked like a WTI number was the Brent settlement, correctly labelled.
- **A clerk's page title can carry the identifiers its body refuses to serve.** Roll Call 317 and H.Res. 1655 came off the title alone.
- **Our own premise can be the error.** We briefed a verifier that Peltola was in Alaska's House race; she is in the Senate race. Put the premise in the verifier's brief so it can be contradicted.
- **An index listing is not a page.** The French MFA's "G7 statement - draft - REV 2" entry resolves to a generic landing page; a listing proves a string existed, not that a document does.
- **A result published after the close cannot explain a move during it.** SoftBank's Q2 loss came out after Tokyo shut; the +13.9% close tracked Monday's US rebound. Check the publication time against the session before writing any "shares rose despite" sentence.
- **Check the verb in the primary.** Google "introduced" four TPU systems in a year; the aggregators said "shipped". One is a product launch cadence, the other a supply claim.
- **A "consensus" can be the economists' own workaround.** The 3.9% October CPI annual rate is estimated by economists because BLS will not publish the October 2025 base month; the Reuters poll covers only the 0.3% monthly. There may be no official y/y figure at all.
- **Announcing an intention to file as privileged is not having it ruled privileged.** The chair rules; the filer claims. Same trap as provisional-v-final.
- **A bloc agreeing on a name is not an election.** Kahana is agreed, nominated by one party, and the outgoing speaker still presides.
- **A sidebar headline we think we saw may not exist.** Our "WLBT sidebar says 2,500" did not survive a re-fetch - the body and the bullet both say "more than 2,000".
- **Vendor benchmarks are vendor-reported, and "early testing" is the lab's own hedge.** Print both.
- **A valuation that appears in no filing is not a filing figure.** SoftBank's US$99.6bn OpenAI stake is in no published SoftBank document; SVF2's whole portfolio is US$136.4bn fair value.
- **A company's own newsroom can be the primary for its own scandal.** Nvidia published both the board statement and the CEO's email to staff. Check the newsroom before trusting anyone's coverage of it - and when you do, the coverage's framing ("possible Chinese spy") is usually absent from the document.
- **An index-page summary can carry the figure the article body refuses to serve.** Three Focus Taiwan article bodies 500'd; the index gave the close, the points and the turnover.
- **A backstop announced is a backstop that worked.** The stabilisation fund authorised intervention the day BEFORE the rally and may have spent nothing. Check whether the trigger predates the move you are explaining.
- **Provisional, final and certified are three states, not one.** A results dashboard still stamped "provisional" is not the declaration, and no source saying "certified" means nobody has certified.
- **Two seat totals can both be right on different bases** - contested seats versus all seats including those filled by nomination. Find what each base excludes before calling either wrong.
- **A "rejection" can be partial and geographic.** China prohibited the CHINA portion of the ABB robotics acquisition, via a security screen rather than the competition regulator, and the deal is still pending. Ask what exactly was blocked, by whom, and what survives.
- **An ellipsis in a quote handed to you is a splice until proved otherwise.** Print the clause you can see whole.
- **A headline's adjective is not the speaker's**: "unpopular" for "isn't necessarily popular". Nor is a headline's paraphrase a quote - the nuclear-threat framing was never in Rubio's words.
- **When officials publish a first name and an initial, that is the name.** A wire's rendering of the surname is not official confirmation, and a cell leader is not a cartel leader.
- **A timestamp in an unstated time zone cannot establish a close.** An 01:00 GMT figure for a market that closes at 08:00 GMT is an early-session reading, whatever the page calls it.
- An order that records no vote is not unanimous. "No recorded dissent" is the only printable form.
- A case number needs the court's own page. A CourtListener id is not a case number.
- A whole litigation cluster can be a conflation. No docket, no agency release, no court page: no story.
- Operative terms live in the order, not the opinion.
- Check the unit, not just the number: "130 people signed up to testify" became "1,300+ comments".
- A referendum inverts the verb. And a ballot measure cannot retroactively govern the election it appeared on.
- Truncated is not rounded, and a different denominator is not a different count.
- A competing estimate is usually a different universe. Reconcile the universe before adjudicating the number.
- A rule names an office, not the person you expect.
- A co-presidency can be real; do not assume a single chair.
- "Record" needs its own series check, and check whether the benchmark is the subject's own previous worst.
- Competing third-party figures are different bases, not different facts. A figure that reconciles to nothing is wrong, not a rival.
- An implausible magnitude is a flag, not a datapoint. A guidance growth rate must reconcile to its base.
- An advocacy publisher's citation may not be what it claims. Follow the footnote.
- A biography needs its own verifier and its own do-not-print list.
- A translation is not a quote, and two translations of one statement are not two sources. One wire with twenty carriers is one source.
- Every quote that breaks, breaks the same way: a real phrase lifted from a longer sentence whose opening clause or trailing qualifier changed its force. Ask for it in two halves.
- A superlative must name its benchmark - among what, over what period, on which index. ("Since June 2022", not "in more than four years".)
- Count the present, not the chamber.
- A party's account of its own operations is not confirmation, and two arms of the same state can contradict each other.
- An institution's index can be stale while the decisive item is live at its own URL. An absence has a shelf life of hours.
- Secondary v filing: the filing wins. Secondary v itself: print neither. Label every number - intraday v close v settlement, GAAP v adjusted, QoQ v YoY, announced v closed, cash-flow v accrual, projection v count.

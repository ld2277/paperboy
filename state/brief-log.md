## STANDING CORRECTIONS - carry forever
- An order that records no vote is not unanimous. "No recorded dissent" is the only printable form; "9-0" on an unsigned interim order is an invention.
- A case number needs the court's own page. ca9.uscourts.gov/opinions beats every outlet; a CourtListener id is not a case number; "Case No. 3AN-26-___" means there is none yet.
- A whole litigation cluster can be a conflation. A confidently described suit, judge, date and two quotes existed nowhere; the source was a different case decided the same day. No docket, no agency release, no court page: no story.
- Operative terms live in the order, not the opinion. A TRO's duration, notice and barred activities come from the order; cite each sentence to the document that holds it.
- Check the unit, not just the number: "130 people signed up to testify" became "1,300+ comments, mostly opposed".
- A referendum inverts the verb - 62-38 against a map means the measure FAILED. Ask what a Yes vote would have done. And a ballot measure cannot retroactively govern the election it appeared on: check causal direction before writing "which is why".
- Truncated is not rounded (48.02 v 48.03), and a different denominator is not a different count (write-ins). One decimal place dissolves both.
- A competing estimate is usually a different universe - mail-only v mail-plus-affidavit, processed v outstanding. Reconcile the universe before adjudicating the number.
- A rule names an office, not the person you expect: five signatures compel a meeting called by the POLICY COMMITTEE chair, not the Conference chair.
- A co-presidency can be real. COP31 has one country's President and another's President of Negotiations, both in UN documents. Do not assume a single chair, and do not credit a wire with a phrase the primary itself uses.
- "Record" needs its own series check - Berkshire's $377.4bn was below its own March figure - and check whether the benchmark is the subject's own previous worst (the ANC's 2021 result).
- Competing third-party figures are different bases, not different facts. Find the arithmetic that reproduces each exactly, say which basis the series uses, never switch silently. A figure that reconciles to nothing is wrong, not a rival.
- An implausible magnitude is a flag, not a datapoint: a 74.5% operating margin, a 146.8% annual growth rate, a 1,206% revenue rise. Compute the implied ratio, then source it to the primary or attribute it to whoever published it. And a guidance growth rate must reconcile to its base - $108bn on $57.0bn is +89%.
- An advocacy publisher's citation may not be what it claims. Follow the footnote: one "quote from his account" was a screenshot posted by an anonymous third party, and an interview dated June was dated July in the advocate's own link.
- A biography needs its own verifier and its own do-not-print list. This run: an expected birth-year dispute did not exist because no institution publishes a birth date at all; an appointment year is not an office year; three sources gave a soldier's unit three ways, so name no unit; and the most quotable episode had no reachable source and was cut.
- A translation is not a quote, and two translations of one statement are not two sources. One wire with twenty carriers is one source. Say who translated; never reproduce a splice of two non-adjacent sentences.
- Every quote that breaks, breaks the same way: a real phrase lifted from a longer sentence whose opening clause, conditional or trailing qualifier changed its force. Pull the full sentence, ask for it in two halves, and watch for a dropped "But" or "First," and for a subordinate clause dressed as a sentence.
- A superlative must name its benchmark - among what, over what period, on which index. Brent "since the pandemic" and WTI "since 2022" are both right; "oil fell X%" made them look irreconcilable.
- A headline's legal word is looser than the body's: a TRO is not a merits ruling, a brief order is not an opinion, a briefing request is not an investigation, a lead is not a win, an estimate is not a called tally, precincts reporting is not expected vote, a conditional commitment is not a loan.
- Count the present, not the chamber. 460 votants in a 577-seat house is not unanimity. When a vote total and a chamber size disagree, that is the question, not a rounding.
- A party's account of its own operations is not confirmation - CENTCOM's, the IRGC's, a health ministry's toll from a strike it blames on its enemy. And two arms of the same state can contradict each other: report the discrepancy and name the institution.
- An institution's index can be stale while the decisive item is live at its own URL. Search the site. An absence is printable only when you have searched, and an absence has a shelf life of hours - re-check at publication time.
- Secondary v filing: the filing wins. Secondary v itself: print neither. Label every number - intraday v close v settlement, GAAP v adjusted, release v call, QoQ v YoY, announced v closed, committed v priced v raised, cash-flow v accrual, projection v count.
- A mid-count snapshot is not a competing final figure, and a page-update time is not a data time. When two counts differ, ask which stopped counting first.
- Run the arithmetic yourself when no outlet has. A seat allocation, a share, a three-line sum or a transfer chain is checkable from the primary table. Do it, and say the calculation is ours.
- A conditional is load-bearing and the easiest thing to lose: "up to", "at least", "expected", "recommended", "pending approvals", "subject to Member States' agreement", "substantially alter or demolish". Dropping it can invert the provision.
- Derive a percentage from the prior close, never from a displayed net change, and check the two agree. An index owner's own page can be stale and self-contradictory; a page's furniture can contradict its body, and the body wins.
- A 404 can be a slug, not an absence - try the obvious variant once. WebFetch hard-truncates a PDF at ~112,000 characters, so fetch long filings at several offsets. EDGAR does not accept weekend filings.
- The weekly PDF strands section labels alone on a page, and `break-before: avoid` does nothing in WeasyPrint. The fix is structural in `tools/paperboy_render.py`: wrap each section's label with its first item in one `break-inside: avoid` container. Needs one manual commit - a run cannot push.
- The renderer escapes any field with no `<` in it (`_para`), so write literal unicode in plain fields and entities only in fields that already carry a tag; never a bare `&` in a tagged field. Entry point `render_issue(brief)`. A field carrying an apostrophe needs double-quote delimiters, one carrying a double quote needs single.

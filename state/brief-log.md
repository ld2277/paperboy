## STANDING CORRECTIONS - carry forever
- **Every manufactured quote this project has caught made the quoted string MORE specific than the source** - an extra adjective, a suffixed entity, a prefix on a verb. **SUSPECT ANY QUOTE OR NAME THAT ARRIVES MORE PRECISE THAN THE DOCUMENT WOULD WRITE IT.**
- **An entity name gains a suffix the same way a quote gains a word.** "Google" is not "Google LLC" and neither is GV. "Led by" is not "co-led by".
- **Three counters measuring three things are not three estimates of one thing.** Name the counter and the capture time, or the figures read as mutual corrections.
- **A docket is the only source for a case name**, and a court's own page is the only source for what it granted.
- **An official's characterisation after the event is not the engineering finding**, however senior the official and however quotable the line.
- A condition spoken before a data release is a tripwire, not a reaction. Check the clocks.
- A quote clipped of its venue or date clause is a different claim.
- A speech index that lists no speech is evidence there was none.
- A politician's insult is a factual claim and verifies like one.
- A board passes capital appropriations, not a spending budget. Write "authorised up to".
- A 10% round-up is an error, not a rounding.
- Two renderings of one wire at different timestamps are two intraday snapshots, not two sources.
- A referral to committee is not a motion to table. Get the chamber's question, not the headline's verb.
- A suspicion is a reason to check, not to cut.
- A clerk's page title can carry the identifiers its body refuses to serve - and so can an x.com page title.
- Our own premise can be the error. Put the premise in the verifier's brief so it can be contradicted.
- An index listing is not a page.
- A result published after the close cannot explain a move during it.
- Check the verb in the primary. "Introduced" is not "shipped".
- A "consensus" can be the economists' own workaround for a missing official series.
- Announcing an intention to file as privileged is not having it ruled privileged.
- A bloc agreeing on a name is not an election. (Kahana is still agreed, not elected.)
- A sidebar headline we think we saw may not exist.
- Vendor benchmarks are vendor-reported, and "early testing" is the lab's own hedge.
- A valuation that appears in no filing is not a filing figure.
- A company's own newsroom can be the primary for its own scandal.
- An index-page summary can carry the figure the article body refuses to serve.
- A backstop announced is a backstop that worked. Check whether the trigger predates the move.
- Provisional, final and certified are three states, not one.
- Two seat totals can both be right on different bases.
- A "rejection" can be partial and geographic.
- An ellipsis or a bracket in a quote handed to you is a splice until proved otherwise.
- A headline's adjective is not the speaker's, and a headline's paraphrase is not a quote.
- When officials publish a first name and an initial, that is the name.
- A timestamp in an unstated time zone cannot establish a close.
- An order that records no vote is not unanimous.
- A whole litigation cluster can be a conflation. No docket, no agency release, no court page: no story.
- Operative terms live in the order, not the opinion.
- Check the unit, not just the number.
- A referendum inverts the verb.
- Truncated is not rounded, and a different denominator is not a different count.
- A competing estimate is usually a different universe.
- A rule names an office, not the person you expect.
- A co-presidency can be real; do not assume a single chair.
- "Record" needs its own series check - and a word that appears only in a headline is the headline's, not the reporting's.
- An implausible magnitude is a flag, not a datapoint.
- An advocacy publisher's citation may not be what it claims. Follow the footnote.
- A biography needs its own verifier and its own do-not-print list.
- A translation is not a quote. One wire with twenty carriers is one source, and one paywalled original behind several relays is also one source.
- Every quote that breaks, breaks the same way: a real phrase lifted from a longer sentence whose opening clause or trailing qualifier changed its force. **Ask for it in two halves.**

AI

Anthropic's filing: $70 to $78 a share, and $900bn of committed compute

What happened

Amendment No. 2 to Anthropic's Form S-1, filed Tuesday under registration 333-299633, sets a price range of $70 to $78 a share on 825,000,000 Class A shares, plus a 123,750,000-share underwriters' option. It lists on Nasdaq as ANT. Q3 revenue was $17.0bn, up 1,127% year-on-year. The number to hold on to sits further down: about $900.9bn of aggregate committed spending, substantially all compute and infrastructure, roughly $451.6bn of it falling before the end of 2030. Net proceeds come to about $84.6bn at the midpoint, $93.7bn with the option exercised, both including a separate $26bn private placement (filing).

What it means

Nine hundred billion of obligations against seventeen billion of quarterly revenue is the AI-capex argument compressed into one document. The range sits well below the $1.5-2tn investors had discussed. The widely quoted $1.3tn valuation and the 18 November pricing date are in the coverage, not the filing. (Anthropic makes the model that writes this brief.)

TSMC's board authorises up to $1bn into the two labs it manufactures for

What happened

At Tuesday's board meeting TSMC authorised up to $500m in Anthropic, to be bought at the IPO price, and up to $500m in OpenAI in preferred stock, alongside capital appropriations of about $40.9bn (MarketScreener, TechNode). Two labels matter. "Up to" caps an authorisation; nothing is closed, and the Anthropic leg depends on an IPO price that does not yet exist. And a board passes capital appropriations, which authorise spending across periods -- not an annual capex budget, which is how one wire headlined it. We did not reach the resolution itself.

What it means

Nvidia and AMD taking stakes in their own customers was already a circular-financing story. The foundry doing it is new, and it puts TSMC on both sides of its own order book. The $40.9bn is the bigger number and got the smaller headline.

Eight governments and four labs sign up to shutdown mechanisms, with no deadlines and no sanctions

What happened

The Paris Call for AI Safety and Security was adopted at the Elysee on 9 November -- a day outside our window, but it only reached coverage Tuesday. Eight governments signed, alongside OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Mistral, with Yoshua Bengio and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent present. Signatories commit to shared evaluation procedures, publishing non-sensitive test results in a standard format, risk-proportionate independent audits, and shutdown mechanisms for models under their control. Germany did not sign. The text is a political self-commitment that, as one account puts it, "sets neither deadlines nor sanctions" (elysee.fr).

What it means

The first time frontier labs have put a shutdown commitment into a signed international text. Unenforceable -- but unenforceable commitments are what later regulation gets drafted against. Germany's absence is the awkward part: the EU's largest economy declining to sign a European declaration.

Nvidia's board hires an outside prosecutor, and the chip numbers get bigger

What happened

Nvidia's newsroom carried a one-sentence statement Tuesday: the independent directors have retained Joon H. Kim, a former acting US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, "to conduct a thorough and independent investigation". It names neither Jensen Huang nor a scope. What it does is route the inquiry around lead independent director Stephen Neal, whom people familiar had told the Financial Times would probably run it: Neal led Cooley from 2001 to 2008, is still its senior counsel, and Cooley has been Nvidia's principal outside counsel since 1993. Separately, The Information reported, citing two people who know her, that Huang's relationship with Alice Huang helped move at least $7.5bn of chips -- some 240,000 H100, H200 and A100-class parts -- to buyers in China; Reuters said it could not verify it. Senator Kevin Cramer, a senior Republican on Senate Banking, told Politico: "I just think he's got to go." The shares fell 5.47%, and are down about 9.5% since the affair became public.

What it means

The Cooley conflict is why the hire is the story: a board paying Wachtell rates to investigate its own founder is buying distance from its own law firm. The one-sentence statement with no stated scope is the tell -- nothing has been conceded, including what is being looked into.

Business

A hot inflation print that cannot say how hot, year on year

What happened

The October CPI landed on schedule Tuesday at 8.30 a.m. Eastern. All items rose 0.4% on the month, seasonally adjusted, after 0.6% in September; core -- all items less food and energy -- rose 0.3%, as in each of the two months before. Energy added 0.7%, food 0.5%, shelter 0.3%. What is missing is the number everyone quotes: BLS "did not collect survey data for October 2025 because of a lapse in appropriations", so for most series it cannot publish a 12-month change at all, and the tables print a dash where the headline rate goes. A few components survive: gasoline +37.4%, new vehicles +0.7%, used cars and trucks -3.3%, unadjusted.

What it means

Anyone citing a US annual inflation rate for October is citing an estimate, not a measurement. What is left is the monthly run-rate, and 0.4% a month does not annualise anywhere near target.

Waller set a tripwire hours before the print, and the print hit it

What happened

Governor Christopher Waller told the Bank of England Watchers' Conference in London on Tuesday morning that if "the October CPI inflation data out later today are similar to the past two monthly readings" -- core around 0.3% "or even higher" -- then "I believe a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting" (speech text). He added: "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take." Core printed 0.3% hours later. The range is 3.75% to 4.00% after the 28 October hold, decided 10-2, Cook and Hammack both dissenting for a quarter-point rise.

What it means

A governor naming his condition in public and the condition being met the same afternoon is about as explicit as this Fed gets without a decision. Two members already wanted a hike before this print. A half-point move is now a live possibility, not a thought experiment. One thing we are not printing: a circulating set of Lorie Logan quotes dated to Tuesday belongs to an October speech and a 2024 conference.

Mercor announces $750m at a reported $20bn

What happened

Mercor, which sells AI training data and agent evaluation to enterprises, announced a $750m Series D at a $20bn valuation (SiliconAngle). Announced rather than confirmed closed; we have not verified a lead investor or how it splits between primary and secondary. Several outlets carry it, so it is not single-sourced, but every specific is headline-level.

What it means

Evaluation is becoming its own layer of the stack. If models converge on capability, the buyer's problem shifts to proving which one is better at their particular job -- and that is a service you can charge for.

Alta raises $47.5m to put a compliance layer under stablecoin settlement

What happened

Alta -- formerly Metal, building its own layer-1 chain with bank-facing compliance built in -- announced a $47.5m Series A on Tuesday led by Index Ventures, with GV, Blackbird, Square Peg and Capital49, the fund set up by Airwallex's co-founders (Fortune; the headline says $48m, the body $47.5m). No valuation disclosed. Twenty-five staff, San Francisco and Melbourne. Chief executive Loong Wang: "We often say we wouldn't have built an L-1 if we didn't have to."

What it means

The interesting detail is who is paying. A payments company's founders funding the chain their own industry would have to settle on is a bet that the compliance layer, not the chain, is the scarce asset.

US

Jordan takes the House Republican leadership; Thune keeps the Senate's by voice vote

What happened

House Republicans, by secret ballot behind closed doors Tuesday, chose Jim Jordan over Steve Scalise to lead them in the minority from 3 January -- 106 to 73, according to members in the room, since the conference publishes no tally (Roll Call). Tom Emmer held off August Pfluger for whip, no margin reported anywhere; Lisa McClain kept the conference chair against two challengers; Mike Johnson did not stand. In the Senate, John Thune and his slate came back by voice vote, so there are no counts at all: Barrasso whip, Tom Cotton conference chair, Capito at policy, Bernie Moreno newly picked for the NRSC (Roll Call). Five senators led by Ron Johnson pushed the start back a few hours and changed nothing.

What it means

The House picked its most confrontational available option for opposition; the Senate picked continuity after losing seven seats, and did it on a voice vote so nobody has to own it. Neither conference has yet held anyone responsible for 3 November.

Hegseth's impeachment articles go to committee, 211-204

What happened

The House disposed of Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Tuesday by referring them to the Judiciary Committee, 211 to 204 -- Roll Call 317, H.Res. 1655 (Stars and Stripes). That is a referral, not a motion to table, though several outlets called it a tabling; the effect is the same, which is that nobody voted on the merits. Massie was the only Republican to oppose it. Al Jazeera, alone, counts eighteen members not voting, eleven of them Democrats -- with full attendance it would have failed. Hegseth: "I stand behind every one of these operations. I was proud to execute them, and I would do it again tomorrow." No source says Speaker Johnson ruled the resolution privileged, so we do not write that he did.

What it means

Worth correcting one thing both the president and the secretary said: they called Massie a defeated former congressman. He is neither. He lost the Kentucky Republican primary on 19 May, 54.9% to 45.1%, and holds his seat until 3 January. A politician's insult is still a factual claim.

World Politics

A second day in Abu Dhabi, a prisoner exchange proposed rather than agreed, and 163 drones overnight

What happened

Ukrainian, Russian and American negotiators met in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and reconvened Wednesday. Ukrinform, attributing Volodymyr Zelensky, says the possibility of a large exchange was discussed; the Kyiv Post has the two sides weighing up to 1,500 prisoners each (Kyiv Post). Two Ukrainian outlets reported it as agreed, one hedging to "reportedly", and nothing else supports that. Moscow raised territorial claims; Ukraine rejected a US plan to redraw Donbas borders. Overnight, Russia launched 163 strike drones and one Kh-59/69 missile, 91 of the drones jet-powered Shahed variants; the Ukrainian Air Force's account of an attack on itself puts 130 drones and 14 Banderol units stopped and 17 locations hit (its own 8 a.m. count). Odesa region lost power to 135,000 consumers and a woman of 70 died of her injuries. Moldova said two Banderol munitions crossed its airspace at 6.04 and 6.06 a.m.

What it means

Talks on prisoners and guarantees while the southern grid takes its heaviest night of the week. Nothing in the readouts touches a ceasefire. Moldova on the flight path twice in three minutes is the new fact. The G7 ministers in Paris, separately, finished Tuesday with a 12-page Ukraine statement reportedly adopted but unreleased -- and an unpublished statement cannot be quoted.

Another tanker struck off Oman, and an ADNOC ship damaged inside UAE waters

What happened

Two fresh incidents Tuesday. A vessel operated by ADNOC Logistics & Services was damaged in an attack inside UAE territorial waters; and UKMTO's Warning 201-26 reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile 19 nautical miles north-east of Lima, Oman, at 13.38 UTC, the master reporting all crew safe (gCaptain). No names, flags, owners or cargoes disclosed for either, nobody named as responsible, and it is not known whether the two are linked. The industry tally: Hormuz stays at severe, 177 security incidents since 1 March, daily transits down to single or low double digits against about 138 a day in 2025.

What it means

A ship hit inside Emirati waters is a different order of thing from one hit in the strait, and the UAE is the party least able to shrug it off. The transit count is the real measure: whoever is doing this has cut traffic through Hormuz by something like ninety per cent without ever closing it.

Israel: a Speaker nominated, a vote set for next Tuesday, coalition talks cancelled

What happened

The Knesset still has not voted on a Speaker. Matan Kahana of Yashar is the Eisenkot bloc's agreed nominee -- note the first name, which we had wrong yesterday -- and 67 MKs have signed a demand to replace the incumbent, Amir Ohana, with Ynet putting the vote next Tuesday (Ynet; Times of Israel). Separately Yashar -- the party, not Gadi Eisenkot personally, as some accounts had it -- cancelled scheduled coalition talks with Likud over what it called Netanyahu's divisive speech in the chamber.

What it means

Eisenkot can assemble 67 votes on a procedural question and still not form a government, which is the difficulty in one line: the anti-Netanyahu majority agrees on who holds the chair and not on who holds the cabinet.

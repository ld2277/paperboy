AI

Nvidia's board met on Huang's future and announced nothing

What happened

Nvidia's board met on Wednesday to consider the future of co-founder and chief executive Jensen Huang, and by Thursday morning had announced no decision. The Wall Street Journal reported it was preparing to oust him; Reuters, citing one person familiar, confirmed only that the board met, and Bloomberg that it is working with an outside firm on replacements (Reuters copy via Investing.com, The Next Web). Nvidia's newsroom carried nothing dated 11 or 12 November at 11:50 UTC. Shares closed Wednesday at $173.73, up 2.87%, then fell about 8.6% after hours to around $158.78 by 8 p.m. Eastern. The New York Times, citing five current and former employees, reported a second relationship, with a woman said to be among Huang's direct reports; asked by telephone, he said, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly" (IBTimes UK). We are not naming her - every outlet with the name traces back to the Times.

What it means

A board that sits all evening and says nothing has not decided. Markets are pricing the exit regardless - some $600bn of value gone since the inquiry became public - and earnings land in five days with no settled answer about who hosts the call.

Q3 FY2027 results, Tuesday 17 November, 2 p.m. Pacific. On paper a change of chief executive looks like an Item 5.02 filing.

Anthropic's IPO arrives with $26bn of its own suppliers' money

What happened

Anthropic's amended registration names seven companies that have agreed to buy $26.0bn of nonvoting Class N stock at the IPO price in a concurrent private placement: Google and Nvidia $10bn each, AMD $2.5bn, $1bn apiece from Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft, and $500m from TSMC Partners (Amendment No. 2 to Anthropic's S-1). Google's leg part-funds obligations it had already committed to, the filing says; no such offset is recorded for the others. The offering itself is 825,000,000 Class A shares at $70-78, all but 23.6m of them sold by the company. No pricing date appears anywhere in the filing; CNBC, citing people familiar, reported pricing on 18 November and a debut on the 19th.

What it means

Anthropic's biggest suppliers are buying the stock of their biggest customer at a price they help set. And the valuations in circulation - $1.02tn, $1.3tn, $1.4tn - are in no filing: they are outlets picking different share counts against one price range (Forbes).

Watch next

Pricing, reported for 18 November. The final prospectus fills in the date the current one leaves blank.

OpenAI's quarter, as described by documents it did not publish

What happened

OpenAI's revenue reached about $10bn in the third quarter, up roughly 50% from about $6.7bn in the second, while its adjusted operating loss widened to about $10.9bn from $10.1bn, according to internal documents reviewed by the Wall Street Journal (Reuters, relaying the Journal). Adjusted operating margin was minus 109%, against minus 150% a quarter earlier. Bloomberg separately put revenue near $10bn, citing a person familiar; the loss and margin rest on the Journal alone. Every figure is adjusted rather than GAAP, and unaudited. OpenAI did not comment.

What it means

The loss is growing in dollars and shrinking against revenue, which is the most flattering true thing available to say about it. It is also the last quarter OpenAI can present on its own terms; audited figures arrive with a prospectus.

Whether OpenAI files publicly in 2027, as its chief financial officer told staff in August it would.

Chip stocks had their best day of the year, hours before the news broke

What happened

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed Wednesday at 10,564.11, up 565.97 points or 5.66%, on Nasdaq's own index page - a figure that had eluded us for three issues. The driver was CoreWeave, which reported on Tuesday after the close: revenue of $3.661bn against $1.365bn a year earlier, a revenue backlog of about $132.5bn, and full-year guidance raised to $13.0-13.3bn (CoreWeave's release). It closed Wednesday at $60.77, up 24.71% (StockTitan's Wednesday tape). Less remarked on, further down the release: a GAAP net loss of $709m against $110m a year earlier, driven by $846m of net interest expense, against GAAP operating income of $207m. Micron closed up 11.89% and AMD up 7.32%.

What it means

The backlog is the number the market bought; the interest expense is the one it skipped. CoreWeave borrows to build capacity it has already sold, which works as long as the buyers keep paying - and they are the same handful of firms now exposed to Nvidia.

Watch next

Nvidia on 17 November, the only print that can show whether that backlog is demand or circulation.

Foxconn's record quarter, then Taipei read the Nvidia headlines

What happened

Hon Hai Precision - Foxconn - reported third-quarter net profit of NT$77.5bn, about US$2.4bn and up 34% year-on-year, a quarterly record, against a Bloomberg survey consensus of NT$70.3bn (Malay Mail). Chairman Young Liu said AI servers "have become Foxconn's most important growth driver" and warned demand would keep outrunning supply into next year; the finance chief said revenue, operating income, net income and earnings per share all set quarterly records. Quarterly revenue was NT$3.03tn, up 47%, from the company's 5 October revenue release. Then the mood turned: the Taiex closed Thursday at 43,091.87, down 1,165.28 points or 2.63%, on NT$1.01tn of turnover, the fall attributed to the Nvidia reports (Focus Taiwan).

What it means

Two sessions, one supply chain, opposite directions. Wednesday priced the demand Foxconn is describing; Thursday priced the governance of the company that generates it. Nothing about the order book changed in between, which is the useful thing to notice.

The Bank of Canada already hiked, and says it may not be finished

What happened

The Bank of Canada's summary of deliberations, published 10 November, shows Governing Council weighing a hold at 2.25% against a rise to 2.50% on 28 October - and it raised, the first increase after six consecutive holds (the Bank of Canada's own rate table). The summary says there "could be a need to increase the policy rate further" and that decisions would be taken "one meeting at a time". The Bank publishes no vote split, and its summary page would not serve us its body text, so those fragments come from coverage of the summary rather than the primary.

What it means

Canada has done the thing the Fed is still arguing about. Waller's case for a 50 basis point hike in December reads differently once a G7 central bank facing the same oil-driven inflation has already moved and is signalling more.

Watch next

The December FOMC, 8-9 December, which comes with a new Summary of Economic Projections.

Garage raises $41m to make fire engines tradeable

What happened

Garage, a New York marketplace for used heavy municipal equipment - fire engines, sanitation trucks - raised a $41m Series B led by Invus Opportunities, taking total funding to $60m, with no valuation disclosed (The SaaS News). The company wraps AI appraisals, automated logistics, equipment warranties and integrated financing around the sale, and says it serves more than 1,000 communities across all 50 states and Canada. The round is dated 10 November and was announced on the 11th; the copy says Garage "has raised", not that it has closed.

What it means

This is the unglamorous version of a trade running right through this year's hardware market: the money is in making a non-standard secondary asset legible enough to transact - appraisal, warranty, financing - rather than in the asset itself.

US

Mississippi flips, and the next Senate is a 55-seat chamber

What happened

Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, the first Democrat elected to the Senate from the state since 1982. The Associated Press called it on Wednesday, with CNN and CBS concurring (Gray TV, carrying the AP call). Hyde-Smith conceded by telephone that afternoon: "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters" (the Mississippi Free Press). Colom's lead was about 4,400 votes, and all of it is provisional - the Secretary of State's results page, checked Thursday at 11:35 UTC, still lists only the 10 March primaries. Counties must transmit returns within ten days of the election under Miss. Code Ann. 23-15-603, i.e. Friday. CBS makes this the Democrats' eighth flip of the cycle and the next Senate at least 55 to 44, Alaska uncalled.

What it means

Fifty-five is not sixty, so it buys no filibuster. It buys committee gavels, subpoena power and the confirmation calendar from January - which is precisely why the outgoing majority is spending November the way it is.

Watch next

County certification on Friday 13 November. Alaska tabulates on 18 November.

Congress's deadline for Jensen Huang came and went

What happened

Chairmen John Moolenaar of the Select Committee on China, Brian Mast of Foreign Affairs and Rick Crawford of Intelligence wrote to Huang on 5 November asking him to accept a joint, closed-door briefing on 18 November about his contacts with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom they describe as a publicly reported Chinese intelligence asset. The deadline sentence is exact: "Mr. Huang must accept the invitation or provide alternative availability by November 11, 2026" (the chairmen's 5 November release). No committee has said whether he answered. Moolenaar told Fox Business a subpoena was likely if he declined, and Mast's committee was said to be preparing one (TechCrunch) - but none of the three committees' press pages carried one at 11:45 UTC Thursday.

What it means

The subpoena is now the smallest of Nvidia's problems and the hardest to shake off. Removing a chief executive does not retire a committee's questions about what left the company, when, and who signed it off - it just changes who has to answer them.

Watch next

18 November, the date on the invitation, and whether anyone from Nvidia appears.

Senate Republicans spend their last six weeks on judges

What happened

With the chamber changing hands in January, Senate Judiciary approved four judicial nominees on Tuesday and sent them to the floor, with aides putting as many as 25 nominations pending - four appellate, 21 district - and Republicans planning to sit into December (GV Wire). Thune: "We're going to make the most of the time." Two Texas nominees were added last week, one of them a recently departed Justice Department official whose nomination skipped the customary home-state review. Durbin accused Grassley of abandoning usual practice; Grassley answered that "the chairman has always had discretion". Republicans hold 53-47 and need only a simple majority. This rests on a single outlet.

What it means

Lifetime appointments are the only thing a departing majority can make permanent in six weeks, so it will spend them all. The procedural shortcuts matter less than the arithmetic: nothing in the minority's toolkit stops a simple majority that is willing to stay late.

World Politics

Abu Dhabi ends with a prisoner swap and nothing on territory

What happened

Two days of Russian-Ukrainian talks in Abu Dhabi ended on Wednesday with an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange and no movement on territory. Ukraine's Rustem Umerov announced it - "The main result is that we managed to reach an agreement on a large-scale prisoner exchange" - and Russia's Vladimir Medinsky confirmed a "fundamental agreement" with details to follow (the Kyiv Independent, Al Jazeera). Neither side gave a number; TASS alone put the swap two to three weeks out at more than 1,000 people a side. Medinsky separately told Russian media that Russia would recover all the land it claims "one way or another". Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow on Thursday for talks with Putin expected around midday (The Moscow Times), then go on to Dublin, where Zelensky will be on Friday for an informal EU leaders' meeting (the Kyiv Post).

What it means

Prisoners are what these talks can deliver; territory is what they exist for. Moscow's own negotiator spent the same week saying publicly that every inch comes back, which tells you which file is actually open and which is being used to keep the room warm.

Watch next

The Putin meeting on Thursday and the Dublin debrief on Friday. Kushner said the exchange number would be settled Thursday.

123 drones overnight; ten killed across the day

What happened

Russia launched 123 long-range attack drones at Ukraine overnight, 73 of them jet-powered, alongside Iskander-M ballistic, Zircon anti-ship and Banderol cruise missiles and Gerbera decoys. The Ukrainian Air Force said that as of 08:00 it had downed or suppressed 89 drones, 51 of them jet-powered, plus five Banderol or Dan-T - 94 of 123, leaving 29 unaccounted for - with impacts at 17 locations and debris at 18 more (the Kyiv Independent, Interfax-Ukraine). Those are the Air Force's own figures and are not independently confirmed. Regional authorities reported ten killed and 40 injured across Ukraine over the past day - including four killed at a market in Chornomorsk, near Odesa, in a daytime strike on Wednesday rather than the overnight raid.

What it means

The shape of the last fortnight holds: negotiators in one capital, jet-powered Shaheds over another. The jet variants are now the majority of each launch and the harder half to stop, which is why the intercept rate keeps looking better than the damage does.

Watch next

Whether the tempo changes while Witkoff and Kushner are in Moscow.

Iran says the enriched uranium is not leaving

What happened

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, told Lebanon's Al Mayadeen that Iran will not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country, and that no negotiations with Washington are under way (Reuters). The much-quoted "not a single particle" is a wire paraphrase, not his words - RFE/RL renders it as a refusal to remove a single atom. Verbatim is the warning that "Any new US attacks will also be met with targeting US bases far away". At Arlington on Veterans Day, Trump said the nine-month war was already won and he wanted a total victory, "not a 93% victory" - one liveblog, corroborated elsewhere only at headline level (the Times of Israel's liveblog).

What it means

The two positions fit together badly and knowingly: Washington treats the fighting as finished and the nuclear file as the bill; Tehran treats that file as the one thing not for sale. Neither touches the blockade or Hormuz, where the cost actually lands.

Watch next

The full Al Mayadeen broadcast, and whether any channel to Washington is acknowledged by either side.

AI

Nvidia beat by $7bn and then guided to $130bn

What happened

Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8bn ($115,844m), up 103% on the year and 20% on the quarter, against a year-ago $57,006m. Data-centre revenue was $107.6bn, up 110% year-on-year. GAAP diluted earnings were $2.70 a share against $1.30; non-GAAP were $2.68 against $1.27. GAAP gross margin was 74.8%, non-GAAP 74.9%. Fourth-quarter guidance is $130.0bn, plus or minus 3%, with gross margin of 71.0% to 72.0% on both bases — against an LSEG analyst estimate of $125.25bn carried by Reuters. All figures from the company’s own 8-K exhibits: press release and CFO commentary.

What it means

The beat was about $6.7bn against the $109.1bn consensus we printed on Monday, but the guide is the number that matters: a 12% sequential step up, promised by a company with an interim chief executive and an open board investigation. Two caveats belong in the same breath. The CFO commentary says the fourth quarter “includes an additional week”, so $130.0bn is not like-for-like. And non-GAAP earnings sit below GAAP for once, because the reconciliation strips out $1,767m of pre-tax gains on equity securities — an unusual direction of travel, not a weak quarter dressed up.

Kress ran the call; the board says Huang is not coming back

What happened

Colette Kress, interim chief executive and finance chief, took her first earnings call. In the release she is quoted: “Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people,” and “Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater.” Board chair Harvey C. Jones — named as chair in Nvidia’s own 12 November release — said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO” and has retained Heidrick & Struggles: “We want to install the CEO, the permanent CEO, as quickly as possible,” while it “won’t expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person” (CFO Dive, corroborated by Reuters via RTE).

What it means

Jones also gave the call something the 12 November release withheld: a reason. He said Huang resigned after “disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms” — his words on the call, relayed through a transcript and Calcalist, not a filing. That is a different claim from the board’s 8 November statement, which said only that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The company has now made two statements and has never joined them. Claims circulating about affairs, a named foreign agent or an FBI inquiry into Huang rest on no primary document we can reach.

Watch next

Whether the independent committee reports before the search concludes. Jones declined all questions on the resignation.

Semiconductors

Nvidia shipped $2.4bn into China and has written China out of the guide

What happened

Two sentences in the CFO commentary do the work. “Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were $2.4 billion.” And: “We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook.” On the call, Kress went further, saying the company is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data-centre products to China. Source: EX-99.2.

What it means

A $130bn quarter guided with a zero in the China column is the clearest statement yet of how Nvidia now plans: the second-largest market for accelerators has been moved from the forecast to the upside case. Note the scope carefully — Kress’s object was licensed Hopper data-centre products, not AI chips generally, and a Bloomberg headline yesterday widened it to the latter. The filing’s own two sentences point in opposite directions in time: $2.4bn went in during the quarter, and nothing is assumed going out.

Whether the company books any China data-centre compute revenue in the fourth quarter at all, against a guide that assumes none.

The export rule is still not published, five days on

What happened

The Commerce interim final rule on remote access to advanced semiconductors, RIN 0694-AK50, released 16 November, has still not appeared in the Federal Register. We queried the Federal Register API by RIN and separately by agency filtered to on-or-after 16 November: both returned a total count of zero. The rule’s DATES field still reads, verbatim, “This rule is effective [INSERT DATE OF PUBLICATION IN THE FEDERAL REGISTER].” A Nixon Peabody client alert of 17 November says BIS “has indicated” publication and effect on 19 November — a law firm’s relay, and still the only date anyone has.

What it means

A bracket placeholder means unpublished, and an unpublished rule is not in force. Publication starts the 60-day comment clock, the 30-day in-transit savings clause and the 90-day window for existing data centres — none of which has a start date yet. Anyone building a compliance timetable off 19 November is building it off a law firm’s expectation. One gap we cannot close: the rule cross-references Country Group D:5 without listing it, and the eCFR refuses automated access.

Watch next

federalregister.gov tomorrow. If it publishes on the 19th, every clock in the rule starts then.

Business

Lowe’s grew 9.3% and only 1.8% of it was the existing stores

What happened

Lowe’s reported third-quarter sales of $22,756m, up 9.3% from $20,813m — but comparable sales rose just 1.8%, so almost all the growth is acquired. GAAP diluted earnings were $2.98 a share against $2.88; adjusted $3.10 against $3.06. Both include a $0.34 benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds, which the company flags itself — roughly a ninth of adjusted earnings. Full-year sales guidance rises to about $92.5bn from $92.0bn and comps to about +0.5% from about flat, while operating margin guidance is trimmed to about 11.1% from 11.2%. Source: company release.

What it means

Three different things are moving in three directions, which is why one outlet headlined this as Lowe’s lowering its forecast and that is at best a third of the story. Strip out the tariff refund and adjusted earnings are roughly flat on the year. It is a sixth consecutive quarter of positive comps, which chief executive Marvin Ellison calls “the resiliency of our Total Home strategy” — on a 1.8% comp against 9.3% headline growth, the resilience is doing less work than the acquisitions.

Lowe’s analyst and investor conference in December. Target and TJX report later today, Walmart tomorrow.

Two October data points, pointing opposite ways

What happened

Census reported October advance retail sales up 0.2% on the month, seasonally adjusted and not adjusted for prices, to $748.2bn — with a confidence interval of plus or minus 0.4%, which includes zero. Year-on-year, up 6.8%; excluding motor vehicles and parts, up 0.6% on the month. Separately the Federal Reserve’s G.17 put total industrial production down 0.4% on the month while manufacturing output rose 0.3%, with capacity utilisation at 76.4% and August revised from +0.1% to −0.1%. Sources: Census CB26-181 and Fed G.17.

What it means

The retail number is not statistically distinguishable from no change, which did not stop one outlet headlining it as a rise twice what was expected. And the industrial production pair is two series, not one: the headline fell while factories rose. Neither release gives the Fed a reason to stop raising.

World Politics

The US bombed Iran; Iran bombed Kuwait

What happened

On Tuesday evening American jets struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz — Iranian territory. An unnamed US military official briefed reporters that the strike was in self-defence and that “US Central Command remains highly vigilant and fully postured to protect Americans across the region,”; there is no on-the-record US statement and no CENTCOM release. Ahmad Nafisi, deputy governor of Hormozgan province, told IRNA the attack “caused no damage to residential, commercial, or infrastructural areas”; IRNA reported five to six explosions. Before dawn today Iran attacked Kuwait — a second Gulf state. Kuwait’s army said on X only that “any explosion sounds heard are the result of air defence systems intercepting hostile attacks”, naming nobody; hours later the foreign ministry named Iran and said “the continuation of these attacks constitutes a dangerous escalation that threatens the region’s security and stability”. Iran’s army claimed via state television that its drones hit Patriot batteries at Ali Al-Salem air base — its own account of its own operation, and nobody else reports damage. Bahrain, struck for the fourth time in six days, issued two danger alerts. At sea, Iran’s armed forces said three tankers struck naval mines on Tuesday while the US military says it has cleared the main routes; the vessel disabled near Khasab on Monday is the Panamanian-flagged Tara Gas, hit a second time after its crew were first reported safe, with one Indian sailor missing, and an ADNOC vessel was hit on Tuesday night with one crew member missing and eleven wounded.

What it means

This is no longer a pattern of drone attacks on one Gulf state with a quiet American response. It is an exchange, on Iranian soil, spreading across the Arab shore of the Gulf. The gap between what Kuwait’s military said and what its foreign ministry said — “hostile”, then “Iranian”, hours apart — is a government deciding how far to escalate rhetorically. Three days of strikes at sea and still no attribution for a single one; UKMTO’s incident page has been unreachable for three days, so every line credited to it is an outlet’s rendering of a report relayed by UKMTO.

Watch next

War-risk insurance premiums for the strait — the number that shows how this is actually being priced. And whether any US official goes on the record: NBC reports two officials saying most American strikes on Iran have gone unannounced for two months, one of them saying “They are carrying out these strikes and not acknowledging them,”.

Correction: CENTCOM has not been silent

What happened

We have reported for several runs that CENTCOM denied the 13 November Bahrain attack and then said nothing. That is wrong. CENTCOM spoke on the record as an institution on 17 November, on X, saying Iranian drones aimed at a US radar in Bahrain “were intercepted without any damage to U.S. equipment or injury to American personnel” — and going further, calling the IRGC’s claim to have hit the radar “FALSE”. The silence that does hold is narrower: on the 15 November attack, Al Jazeera reported that day that the US military “has yet to comment on Sunday’s claim”, and on today’s Kuwait and Bahrain attacks no CENTCOM, Fifth Fleet or Pentagon statement appears in any of four outlets we checked, with the Pentagon declining to comment to NBC.

What it means

A command that denies one claim outright and declines to address two others is making choices, and “CENTCOM is silent” flattened that into something it was not. Our channels, named: centcom.mil loads but is stale to October 2024, which is worse than dead because it looks alive, and CENTCOM’s own timeline refuses automated reading — so this rests on outlets quoting the posts, not the posts.

Watch next

Any CENTCOM statement on today’s Kuwait attack. Its absence, now, is a reportable fact rather than an assumption.

Trains are crossing the Kerch bridge again; cars are not

What happened

Russia’s transport ministry, via Interfax, says trains on the Crimean bridge’s rail section are running on schedule while road traffic remains “still completely halted”. A motorway section and one arch are reported collapsed after an overnight Flamingo strike, on open-source imagery — with no official Russian confirmation of any collapse. Dmitry Peskov told Izvestia “There is no doubt that the bridge will be restored and will be even better than before.” Overnight, Ukraine’s air force says Russia launched 141 attack drones from 6 p.m., 73 of them jet-powered, and that 126 were downed or suppressed, with hits in seven locations — an interim count, published while the attack was still running. No missiles.

What it means

Rail back in four days is faster than Ukraine will have wanted, and it is the half that matters militarily: the rail deck moves materiel to the southern front. The road section is the one an aviation expert quoted by Kyiv Independent puts at “not just months, but a year or even longer”.

Watch next

Satellite imagery of the arch. The collapse claim will be settled by a picture, not a statement.

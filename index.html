AI

Block says it trained a human-sounding voice model on a single Nvidia rack, and will not show its work

What happened

Block — Jack Dorsey's payments company — unveiled Dessa, a full-duplex speech model, meaning it listens and talks at the same time, and can interrupt or murmur agreement mid-sentence the way people do. Block's own page calls it “the most authentic full-duplex speech model ever built” and says “We trained Dessa from scratch on nothing but human conversation” — no text, no pretrained base — on “only one NVIDIA GB200 NVL72”. Dorsey announced it on 23 October; TechCrunch wrote it up on Saturday. What Block has not published is everything a sceptic would want: no weights, no code, no model card, no benchmarks, no parameter count and no licence. Access is a waitlist.

What it means

Two things are worth separating. The frugality claim is real but smaller than it sounds — a GB200 NVL72 is a rack of 72 GPUs, not one chip, so “one” is doing a lot of work in that sentence. And with no benchmarks at all, every performance claim here is a company talking about itself. The interesting part is the architecture: a speech-to-speech model trained without text is a bet that the text-first pipeline everyone else uses is the thing making voice assistants feel dead.

Watch next

Block promises launch links “soon”. Weights or a benchmark would make this a story; another demo reel would not.

Forced to name one candidate, Grok picked the Republican 92% of the time and GPT the Democrat 97%

What happened

A study by Convovo, a research studio led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, queried Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol across nearly 500 House, Senate and governor races with a prompt that forbade hedging. Per TechCrunch's reporting, Grok named the Republican in 92% of answers and GPT the Democrat in 97%; the two agreed less than 10% of the time. GPT picked the Democrat in 57 of 67 heavily Republican districts; Grok picked the Republican in 47 of 72 deep-blue ones. With web search switched off across 30 races, Grok refused in 27 while GPT still picked the Democrat in 24 — Parikh's reading: “it's in the weights”. OpenAI says ChatGPT “is designed to be objective by default”.

What it means

Treat the numbers with care. Convovo's own project page publishes none of them and its method section is empty, so this is one outlet reporting figures the researcher supplied. The durable finding is the one that needs no statistics: the same question put to two leading models returns opposite answers, and in Grok's case the sources cited were a Christian conservative voter guide, Turning Point Action and X. Pew puts chatbot use for election information at 8% of US adults, which is the only independently checkable figure here.

Watch next

Whether Convovo publishes a method and dataset, and whether either lab says anything before 3 November.

Semiconductors

Korea ran a $116.6bn current account surplus in one quarter — more than Germany and Japan combined

What happened

Korea's second-quarter current account surplus was $116.6bn, up 310% from $28.4bn a year earlier — an increase of $88.2bn. On IMF cross-country data it ranked second of 36 economies behind China, ahead of Germany at $52.3bn and Japan at $50.6bn, whose combined $102.9bn fell $13.7bn short of Korea's. The engine is explicit: semiconductor exports were $113.89bn in the second quarter, up 181.6%, and $148.02bn in the third, up 218.7%. The Bank of Korea's own balance-of-payments releases put the first half at $191.01bn, or 18.8% of GDP, against a full-year record of $123.1bn in 2025.

What it means

This is what the AI buildout looks like in national accounts rather than in a vendor slide. One country is now exporting roughly $38bn of chips a month, and the surplus it throws off in three months exceeds its own annual record. It also makes Korea acutely exposed: Korea University's Kang Sung-jin warned the government “should not formulate policies on the assumption that the semiconductor boom will continue”, and the IMF's own projection path has the surplus fading to $295.7bn by 2029.

Watch next

SK hynix reports on 28 October and Samsung on 29 October. The BOK's $450bn full-year figure is a 22 October assessment carried in the Korean press, not a published forecast — watch for it on paper.

Taiwan's factory output rose 19.17% in September, its 31st straight month of growth

What happened

Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs reported September industrial production up 19.17% year on year to an index of 140.76, the 31st consecutive month of expansion, with the gain attributed to AI demand. Separately the Taiwan Institute of Economic Research put manufacturing sentiment at a 55-month high while attaching risk warnings; Young Optics guided to sequentially lower shipments this quarter (Taipei Times, 24 October).

What it means

Taiwan's industrial production index is one of the few hard, official, monthly reads on whether AI demand is actually being built rather than announced, and it keeps saying yes. Thirty-one months is long enough that the comparison base is no longer easy, which makes a 19% print more impressive than it looks. The TIER sentiment number is the soft counterpart and it is at a four-and-a-half-year high — forward-looking, and therefore the thing that breaks first if hyperscaler capex guidance disappoints next week.

Watch next

Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report next week and none has given a 2027 capex number. Taiwan's October production print lands in late November.

Business

Apollo's chief economist put the AI bear case in one sentence, and the credit market started agreeing

What happened

In notes published across last week, Apollo chief economist Torsten Sløk wrote that “the core economic flaw in the AI bull case is that the marginal cost of producing compute exceeds the marginal benefit it delivers to users, raising questions about claims of unlimited demand for data centers”, and that “so far, the AI payoff for firms has been slow to materialize”. He argues the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside tech, and cites Ramp data showing 80% of the top 1% of AI spenders are venture-backed — i.e. paying compute bills out of fundraising, not revenue. Fortune, collecting the week's notes, adds that Meta's five-year credit default swap hit a record 114.79 basis points and CoreWeave's reached 1,145.

What it means

The capex numbers are not in dispute. Meta's third-quarter filing guides 2026 capital expenditure, including finance-lease principal, to $135–145bn, and gives no 2027 figure at all. What is newly in dispute is who pays if the revenue lags, and credit is where that argument shows up first: CoreWeave's stock is down more than 30% this month. Sløk's “outside of tech” framing is the sharp end — a much broader claim than one about hyperscalers.

Watch next

Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon next week, and whether any of them puts a 2027 number on paper. Fortune names no data provider for either CDS level; treat both as its reporting.

Iran rules out any US naval return to the Gulf as Brent holds near a six-month high

What happened

Major General Ali Abdollahi, chief of staff of Iran's armed forces, said on Saturday that the US president could only dream of American troops entering the Persian Gulf again, and spoke of drawing the US and Israel deeper into a quagmire (Al Jazeera; Iranian state media's own wording differs, so this is a paraphrase rather than a quote). It answered Trump, who told a Florida rally on Friday the US military “may do another Epic Fury”. Brent settled at $116.72 on Friday, a six-month high and up about 6% on the week. Nour News, tied to Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said reopening the Strait of Hormuz is conditional on US concessions.

What it means

The escalation in the window is rhetorical — no OPEC+ decision, no closure, no reopening — but the tanker attrition underneath it is real and badly measured. The IRGC's repeated claim that supertankers struck mines has no third-party corroboration: the Joint Maritime Information Center found “no substantiation”, CENTCOM denies it flatly, and not one vessel has ever been named. UKMTO says it cannot establish the true number because targeted ships are not reporting to it — 135 incidents since 28 February.

Oil opens Monday into an Israeli election on Tuesday, a tightening Fed on 27–28 October and the OPEC+ seven on 1 November.

Instacart has hired a bank and talked to DoorDash; Uber reportedly walked away

What happened

TechCrunch relayed on Saturday that The Information reported Instacart has hired an investment bank to weigh its options and held acquisition talks with DoorDash, and that Bloomberg reported separate talks with Uber from which Uber withdrew. Both outlets describe the discussions as early and possibly leading nowhere. Instacart's market capitalisation is above $13bn and the stock is up 25% this year; it rose more than 9.2% on Friday on the reports while Uber and DoorDash fell. Instacart did not respond to a request for comment, and neither DoorDash nor Uber has commented.

What it means

Nothing here is confirmed by any party, and the original reporting is Friday's — treat it as a scoop being passed along rather than a deal. The logic is still legible: Instacart has spent two years being told it is a grocery-advertising business inside a delivery business, and a sale to the one competitor with national density would settle that argument. That Uber reportedly looked and left is the more informative half.

Watch next

A confirmation, a leak of terms, or the silence that means the talks died. Instacart's next quarter would be the natural venue.

US

A Ninth Circuit panel will hear the California ballot case by Zoom on a Sunday afternoon

What happened

In an order filed on Saturday, a Ninth Circuit panel of Judges Bennett, Bumatay and Sanchez set remote oral argument in United States v. California, No. 26-7070, for “Sunday, October 25, 2026, at 1 p.m. PDT” — 60 minutes, 30 to each side, with California, Secretary of State Shirley Weber and Los Angeles Registrar-Recorder Dean Logan free to divide theirs “however they see fit”. Appellees could file an optional reply by noon the same day. At issue are ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters federal agencies say are likely non-citizens. California's motion to dismiss calls 18 U.S.C. § 611 a “misdemeanor criminal prohibition on individual noncitizen voting” that “does not purport to set any rules governing election administration”.

What it means

Appellate panels do not sit on Sunday afternoons. The calendar is the holding: with the election on 3 November and every mail ballot already sent out by 10 October, the court has decided the question cannot wait for the district judge — who has still not ruled and has his own hearing on 27 October. That is exactly the posture California is attacking as premature, and it is the posture the Purcell principle, which disfavours late changes to election rules, was written for.

Watch next

The ruling, which could come within hours of the argument, then Carter on 27 October. Logan's “unverified allegations” line comes from a county press statement, not his filing.

Florida has already cast 2.33m ballots, Democrats ahead by mail and Republicans in person

What happened

Florida's own vote-by-mail and early-voting counter, timestamped 24 October at 5:02pm, shows 1,320,003 mail ballots returned — 566,514 Democratic, 472,385 Republican, 249,233 unaffiliated, 31,871 other — and 1,007,584 early in-person votes: 446,330 Republican, 364,370 Democratic, 171,955 unaffiliated, 24,929 other. Democrats lead mail returns by 94,129; Republicans lead early in-person by 81,960. The state publishes no combined total; 2,327,587 is our sum of its two columns, and every column adds to its stated subtotal.

What it means

The split is the point, and it is a reversal of the pattern that held through 2020: Republicans now bank votes by walking into a polling place and Democrats by post. That matters legally as well as politically, because almost every live ballot case — California's included — turns on mail ballots, which is now the method the other party's voters disproportionately use. It also means the early numbers tell you about mobilisation and nothing about the result.

Watch next

The counter updates daily to 3 November. Nothing in it is a count of votes for anyone.

World Politics

Brazil votes today in a run-off where the leader depends on which denominator you use

What happened

Three polls landed on Saturday and the answer changes with the arithmetic. Quaest had Flávio Bolsonaro over Lula 51–49 on valid votes, 46–45 on its stimulated total and 44–42 spontaneous — Flávio ahead on every measure (n=3,702, margin 2 points). CNT/MDA showed 46.3–45.0 on the full distribution, which is 50.7–49.3 on valid votes, and its lead widened, not narrowed, from eight days earlier. Futura Inteligência had 50.1–49.9 on valid votes but 46.6–46.1 to Lula in Gazeta do Povo's spontaneous scenario — the only table in which Lula leads, and a single-outlet one. Polls opened at 8am Brasília time, an hour after this was written.

What it means

A race this close is decided by the people the pollsters classify as undecided, blank or spoiled, precisely the group the denominator choice moves in and out of the count. Anyone citing one number today is picking a table. The institutional machinery is running hard: about 25,000 troops in 301 municipalities and an integrity test on voting machines in all 27 federal units.

Watch next

Polls close at 5pm Brasília (8pm UTC) and Brazil counts fast. Meta and X owe the electoral court six-hourly reach reports on two pro-Bolsonaro accounts, with a final one due six hours after close.

Tel Aviv fills up for an October 7 inquiry as Israel's final poll leaves Netanyahu 11 short

What happened

The October Council — survivors, former hostages and bereaved families — rallied at Hostages Square on Saturday night under the title “In Three Days, Vote for a State Commission of Inquiry”, with Democrats chairman Yair Golan marching in from Rabin Square and Gadi Eisenkot, Bennett, Lapid and Liberman attending. Organisers claimed over 100,000 and police offered no estimate; the Hebrew press put it far lower — Ynet and Israel Hayom reported thousands, Davar tens of thousands. Likud petitioned to block television coverage as election propaganda; committee chairman Justice Noam Sohlberg refused for want of a factual basis. Channel 12's final poll, published Saturday evening, gave Eisenkot's Yashar 23 seats and Likud 21, with the anti-Netanyahu bloc on 70 against 50.

What it means

Netanyahu's problem is not one bad poll but four parties sitting under the 3.25% threshold — Winter's at 2.5%, Hendel's at 2.4% — each wasting votes that would otherwise be his. That is why he spent the week buying out small lists rather than campaigning. The inquiry demand unites everything opposite him, and it is a demand about his own conduct.

Watch next

Polling on 27 October, then coalition arithmetic. Eisenkot leads Netanyahu 44–38 on suitability for prime minister.

Albania's protests get a party, and a casualty figure that will not survive contact with the health ministry

What happened

Saturday's protest in Tirana was an unannounced national one at the prime minister's office, fronted by Democratic Party MPs marching from party headquarters, with roughly 2,000 police deployed — a distinct strand from the student occupation now in its twelfth day. Cushions Edi Rama had laid out for his offered dialogue were burned. Al Jazeera reported “more than 300 protesters and police injured, authorities said Saturday”, naming no body. Albania's health ministry puts Saturday's injuries at 23 — six police, 13 protesters, three journalists and one MP. The 320 figure belongs to 13–21 October, cumulatively.

What it means

Worth stating plainly, because the wire number is already being rewritten elsewhere: the daily balances across this wave have run 9, 11, 13, 21 and 23, and nothing supports 300 in a day. Rama blamed Russia and Iran on 19 October, not Saturday; on Saturday he named no states. The real development is structural: an opposition party has put itself at the head of a leaderless revolt, which is usually how one ends.

Watch next

Whether the students reconverge with the party or refuse it. Cumulative arrest totals remain genuinely unresolved — 167, 271 and a police count of 69 arrests with 20 proceedings all circulate, and Tirana's courts have ruled both ways on the same detentions.

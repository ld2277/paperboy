AI

Nvidia and AMD buy into the customer

What happened

Meta's free-writing prospectus of 26 October names Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom as “Cornerstone Investors” who have indicated an interest in buying up to an aggregate of $5.0 billion of Class A stock in its offering. The filing says the indications are not binding and gives no per-company split. The companies filled that in themselves on Tuesday: Nvidia's own release says it will invest $3 billion, AMD's says $1 billion. No Broadcom figure exists in any filing or release. Alongside the money, Meta will expand deployment of Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems, Vera CPUs, Spectrum-X Ethernet and BlueField-4; AMD's Helios racks are installed and being tested at Meta, with a custom Instinct-based accelerator to follow.

What it means

This is the circular financing that has been making credit analysts twitchy, now written into a prospectus: the chip vendors are funding the buyer of the chips. Note what it is not. An indication of interest is not a purchase, “up to” is not a flat amount, and the one number everybody is quoting for Broadcom was produced by subtraction.

Whether the indications convert when the offering settles. Nvidia reports fiscal Q3 in November.

GSA cuts a 40% Claude deal days before the $1 offer lapses

What happened

The General Services Administration announced on Tuesday an expanded OneGov agreement with Anthropic giving federal agencies a 40% discount on token-based usage across Claude models, charged on consumption with no minimum purchase. GSA said the model “scales with actual use” and that it “will renew agreements where appropriate.” The timing is the story: Anthropic's previous $1-per-user government offer expires on 31 October. Claims circulating that the deal extends to state, local and tribal governments, or implies $1bn of savings, appear in an aggregator and not in GSA's own account.

What it means

The $1 offers were customer acquisition; this is the price that follows. A 40% consumption discount with no floor is a much more ordinary piece of procurement, and it tells you roughly what the vendors think federal usage is now worth holding.

Watch next

Whether OpenAI and Google renew their own OneGov terms before 31 October.

DeepMind's Genie 4 generates sound as well as worlds

What happened

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on Tuesday, its next real-time interactive world model and, per the post, “our first real-time world model to generate audio natively” — music, ambient noise and interactive dialogue generated simultaneously with the scene. Gemini models help hold scenes coherent; users steer with keyboard controls and text prompts. It ships as a research preview for trusted testers, coming to Google AI Ultra subscribers via Project Genie and to early-access partners through the Gemini API. DeepMind concedes that “visual details and object permanence can still shift or drift over time.” Widely repeated claims of a two-minute coherence window, double Genie 3, appear nowhere in the post.

What it means

Audio is the part that matters. A world model that only renders pixels is a demo; one that renders a soundscape in step with them is the beginning of a usable simulator for robotics and game development.

Watch next

General availability, and whether anyone outside the trusted-tester set measures the drift.

Semiconductors

SK hynix: 80% operating margin, and memory sold out to 2029

What happened

SK hynix reported third-quarter revenue of KRW 104,615.5 billion — 104.6 trillion won, up 32% on the quarter and 328% on the year — with operating profit up 632% year on year and a company-stated operating margin of 80%, against 76% in Q2 and 47% a year ago. Net profit was KRW 39,775.6 billion, down 58% sequentially. It has long-term agreements with 14 major customers covering more than half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity, says HBM4 will pass HBM3E in volume and revenue in Q4, and expects demand to exceed supply through 2030. DRAM marketing vice-president Park Jun-deok said that over the next three years “virtually all of our D-Ram and high-bandwidth memory volumes are effectively sold out.” Shareholder returns for 2026 come to roughly KRW 100 trillion.

What it means

An 80% operating margin is not a semiconductor number; it is a toll-booth number. Contracts covering half of two years' capacity have turned a famously cyclical business into something closer to an annuity, and handed the cost of the AI buildout straight to the hyperscalers. The 58% drop in net profit is the one line that doesn't fit; it sits below the operating line.

Samsung's full Q3 results, and whether Micron's next guide confirms the sold-out picture.

ASM International raises its 2027 outlook by a quarter

What happened

The Dutch deposition-equipment maker reported Q3 revenue of EUR 1.16 billion against about EUR 1.11 billion expected, adjusted net income of EUR 315.4 million against EUR 206.2 million a year earlier, and a 52% gross margin. It guided Q4 to EUR 1.23 billion and raised its 2027 revenue outlook to more than EUR 5.7 billion at constant currency, from more than EUR 4.6 billion — an uplift of roughly 24% — and added a buyback of up to EUR 300 million. These figures reach us through one aggregator's relay of the company release, not a page we fetched.

What it means

Equipment guidance is the leading indicator memory results are not. Lifting a 2027 number by a quarter, more than a year out, says ASMI's customers have already committed the capacity plans SK hynix just described from the other end.

NXP shows what the rest of the chip industry looks like

What happened

NXP reported Q3 revenue of $3,786 million, up 19% year on year and 8% sequentially, with non-GAAP earnings of $4.20 a share and a Q4 guide midpoint of $3,850 million. Industrial and IoT rose 39% and Comms Infrastructure 54%, but Automotive — still more than half the business — managed 13% and Mobile fell 4%. The shares fell 7.4% after hours. Seagate did better on paper and fared no better: revenue of $4,233 million against $2,629 million a year earlier, a December-quarter guide of $4.8 billion, and a 6% fall.

What it means

Two things are happening at once. Anything attached to an AI datacentre is repriced as scarce. Everything else — cars, phones, industrial — is a normal cyclical business having a decent quarter, and the market has stopped paying for decent quarters.

Business

The Fed decides this afternoon, into hot inflation and a six-day-old election

What happened

The FOMC's two-day meeting ends today, with the statement at 2:00pm Eastern and Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference half an hour later — after this brief. The target range is 3.75–4.00% following September's quarter-point increase, decided 12–0. The data since has not helped: September CPI came in at 3.7% year on year and 0.6% on the month, core at 2.6%, both above expectations. The Conference Board's consumer confidence index rose to 91.7 from a revised 84.2, its first gain in four months, though inflation expectations stayed at 5.8%. Futures showed a hold as overwhelmingly likely.

What it means

A central bank that was raising rates in September, into an election a week away, with inflation accelerating, has almost no comfortable options. Standing still is the one that requires the least explanation, which is usually a good guide to what happens.

Watch next

The statement and Warsh at 2:00 and 2:30pm ET. September PCE lands Thursday. The Bank of Japan decides Friday from 1.25%, and the Fed's next projections come at the 8–9 December meeting.

A narrow rally, and crude takes the refiners down with it

What happened

Tuesday closed with the Nasdaq Composite up 1.18% at 26,477.81 and the S&P 500 up 0.60% at 7,722.60, while the Dow managed 0.03% — breadth was poor and the megacaps did the lifting, Broadcom up 4% and CoreWeave 7.9%. Treasury yields barely moved: 4.70% at two years, 4.99% at ten, 5.30% at thirty. Front-month Brent settled at $113.88, down 1.67% and December WTI at $99.90, and the refiners took the damage — Valero down 9.4%, Marathon Petroleum 7.5%, Phillips 66 7.3%. Boeing fell 10.7% on a $116 million loss. Asia then ran with SK hynix: the Kospi closed at 6,012.44, up 6.63%, its first close above 6,000 in ten sessions.

What it means

A 0.6% index gain in which most members fall is not a rally, it is a rotation into four companies. The oil move is the more interesting one: crude fell hard enough to crush refining margins a week before an election in which pump prices are a live issue.

Brent opened Wednesday back up about 1.2%. Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close.

Visa's record year, and nobody cares

What happened

Visa closed its fiscal year with net revenue of $45.9 billion, up 15%, and fourth-quarter revenue of $12.1 billion, up 13%, on non-GAAP earnings of $3.48 a share. Payments volume rose 9% and cross-border volume 14% in constant currency. The company returned $25.6 billion over the year, including $20.5 billion of buybacks at an average $335.49, and raised the dividend 16% to 78 cents. Chief executive Ryan McInerney called it “a milestone year.” The shares fell about 2% after hours. The release contains no fiscal-2027 guidance at all, despite secondary coverage describing a guidance cut.

What it means

The pattern across Tuesday night was consistent: Visa, Seagate and NXP all beat and all fell. When the index is a percent off its high and four stocks are carrying it, a good quarter from a mature business buys nothing.

Startups

Re:Build raises $2.1bn to re-industrialise, backed by General Catalyst and JPMorgan

What happened

Massachusetts-based Re:Build announced $2.1 billion on Tuesday, co-led by General Catalyst and JPMorganChase's Strategic Investment Group. Neither the round type nor the valuation was disclosed and no other investors were named. Founded in 2020 by Amazon's former commercial chief Jeff Wilke, the company partners with industrial firms on design, engineering and manufacturing at scale, including aerospace and defence, and incubates startups — it spun out the EV maker Slate Auto, which has raised at least $1.4 billion. Its network employs more than 2,100 people across more than 2.4 million square feet. Wilke: “We started Re:Build because the deindustrialization of America is an economic failure and national security risk.”

What it means

A $2.1bn round with no disclosed valuation is a growth-equity deal wearing venture clothing. The interesting signal is the co-lead: a bank's strategic arm putting that much into physical manufacturing is the kind of capital that used to go to private equity roll-ups.

Whether Slate Auto ships, and what Re:Build does with defence demand.

A Dallas firm closes a $10bn fund for AI and defence

What happened

Disruptive, founded in 2012 by Alex Davis, announced a $10 billion fund on Tuesday to back at least ten late-stage companies in AI and defence, with individual cheques as large as $1 billion. It is the firm's first blind-pool fund; it had previously invested deal by deal through special-purpose vehicles. Past positions include Palantir, Airbnb, Spotify, Groq and ElevenLabs. Secondary reporting — relayed rather than confirmed here — has the firm telling limited partners it has returned more than $6 billion on roughly $1.9 billion invested, and puts committed capital before the announcement at $7.5 billion or more. Limited partners were not named.

What it means

Ten companies, $10 billion, late stage: this is not venture capital, it is a concentrated public-equity strategy applied to private companies that will not go public soon. The first blind pool after fourteen years of SPVs is the part that should prompt questions about who the LPs are.

Guidewire pays $1.7bn cash for an agentic underwriting startup

What happened

Insurance-software vendor Guidewire is acquiring Hyperexponential, a UK pricing and underwriting company, for $1.7 billion in cash, with a further $400 million reserved against revenue targets. Founded in 2017 and backed by Andreessen Horowitz and Battery Ventures, Hyperexponential had raised nearly $90 million, and says nearly 600 insurers in 40 countries use its software. The deal is expected to close sometime in 2027. The $400 million is contingent, so the widely quoted “around $2 billion” is a ceiling, not a price.

What it means

Roughly nineteen times capital raised, in cash, for a nine-year-old company selling into one of the slowest-moving software buyers there is. Specialty underwriting is exactly the kind of judgement-heavy, data-poor process where a model earns its keep, and the incumbents have decided to buy rather than build.

US

Judge Carter won't rush, and that may settle the California ballot fight

What happened

US District Judge David O. Carter heard argument in Los Angeles on Tuesday on the Justice Department's request to bar California from counting mail ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters the administration says are likely noncitizens. He did not rule, said he would issue an order governing proceedings by Wednesday, and said he was likely to appoint a special master to examine the government's evidence at a hearing roughly two weeks out — which would put any decision past 3 November. From the bench he said that “some of these may be very valid voters, and that's my concern about disenfranchisement”, told prosecutor Bill Essayli that if the problem were as large as claimed “you've got a remedy for goodness sakes — go out and indict,” and said “any threats to state and county election employees cease immediately.” He declined to “just wing it with a ruling” on evidence filed the night before.

What it means

Delay is the remedy here. Carter has not said the government is wrong; he has said he will not decide this fast, and the calendar does the rest. The Ninth Circuit dismissed the government's appeal for lack of jurisdiction on Monday, so there is no faster route.

Watch next

Carter's order, due today. Then whether a special master is appointed, and against which certification deadline — reporting differs between 3 and 11 December.

The evidence itself is contested, and the same play is running in two more states

What happened

Los Angeles County told the court that a preliminary analysis of a 200-name sample drawn from the Justice Department's list was largely people who say they were born in the United States, most with no voting history and no ballot sent. Essayli told the court federal investigators re-checked the same 200 names and found only four citizens; the county has not confirmed the two reviews covered the same list, and the wire accounts differ on whether the federal sample was even the same size. California's lawyer called the government's approach “mysterious database methods”. Federal prosecutors charged 28 people in California last week over alleged unlawful registration or voting — charges, not convictions. The department has also asked an Alaska court to block certification of the Senate result if the Republican incumbent loses narrowly, which Alaska is moving to dismiss, and wrote to Washington state on Monday asking officials not to count ballots from 400 voters.

What it means

Two irreconcilable readings of one 200-name sample, six days before an election, is the whole dispute in miniature. Neither side's count has been tested by anyone neutral, which is precisely the gap a special master is for.

Watch next

Washington's response, and Alaska's dismissal motion. Congress is doing none of this: the Senate's last recorded roll call was 30 September, and funding runs to 11 December.

World Politics

Israel votes, and nobody wins

What happened

Turnout reached 77.7% at poll close, per the Central Elections Committee — about seven points above 2022 and, on the committee's own framing, the highest at that stage since 2001. The count has moved against Benjamin Netanyahu ever since: at 4:05am his bloc had 63 seats; by 10:58am, with 76.8% of boxes counted, it was 54, against 47 for Gadi Eisenkot's bloc and 19 for the Arab-led parties, outside both. Neither has 61. At the 51.5% mark Likud had 27 seats and Eisenkot's Yashar! 21; Ra'am, below the threshold overnight, is now above it. At Likud headquarters at 3:23am Netanyahu said Israel faces “a historic, one-time opportunity along with our friend President Trump” to remove the Iranian threat. Eisenkot said Netanyahu “is the head of a caretaker government without a mandate of public confidence.”

What it means

The arithmetic is the story. The 19 seats that would settle it belong to parties most of the opposition rules out governing with, and Eisenkot has refused to serve under Netanyahu even on rotation. That leaves a unity government, a defection, or another election — and Ben-Gvir has already called the first a terrible mistake.

Watch next

Some 600,000 to 650,000 double-envelope ballots are still being verified, possibly into Friday. Official results publish 4 November. Herzog then taps an MK, who gets 28 days with one possible 14-day extension. No deadline binds Herzog himself.

The Oleshky ceasefire holds, and spreads

What happened

The local ceasefire in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied city across the Dnipro from Kherson, took effect in the early hours of 27 October to let civilians out and aid in. Ukraine's ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said the first thirty hours passed “without a single incident.” Melania Trump, who brokered it, said on Tuesday it had been “expanded within Kherson Oblast,” and a deputy head of the regional administration confirmed shelling had stopped more widely. The Kyiv Independent reports it runs to 1 November; wire accounts say only into the weekend. The war did not pause elsewhere: two killed and seventeen injured over the day, and the Ukrainian Air Force says Russia sent 88 Shahed drones overnight.

What it means

A humanitarian corridor is not a peace process, and this one covers a city Russia blockaded in the first place. But it is the first arrangement in months both sides have kept for more than a day, and it widened rather than collapsed.

Watch next

Whether it survives the weekend, and whether anyone extends the model to another front.

China's Fifth Plenum is sitting now, on party discipline

What happened

The Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee opened in Beijing on Monday and runs to 29 October, closed-door, chaired by Xi Jinping. The expected output is a decision on “full and rigorous Party self-governance” — discipline, cadre effectiveness, anti-corruption — plus possible leadership changes before the 2027 Party Congress. No communiqué has issued and no expulsions announced. The backdrop is heavy attrition in the Central Committee's military ranks.

What it means

A plenum devoted to internal discipline rather than economic policy is itself the signal: Xi is spending a set-piece occasion on his own party machinery a year out from the Congress that decides its composition.

Watch next

The communiqué, due tomorrow, and any personnel changes in it.

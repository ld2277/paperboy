AI

OpenAI says a model has crossed its own “critical” cyber line

What happened

OpenAI published Path to Astra on 1 September, disclosing a model it says “meets the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold under our Preparedness Framework” and is “the first model we are designating at this level.” Astra scored, in OpenAI's words, “a perfect score of 100% on the benchmark to evaluate the model's ability to develop exploits from known vulnerabilities.” During that evaluation the model “discovered and used two zero-day vulnerabilities as part of an exploit chain”, which OpenAI says it is now disclosing to the maintainers. Access to the most capable version will be gated. Every number here is company-reported; no third party has replicated the evaluations.

What it means

Preparedness thresholds were written as a promise about what OpenAI would not ship without safeguards. This is the first time the company has said a model reached the top rung, which converts an abstract governance document into a live decision about who gets an autonomous exploit-finder. The gating design, not the benchmark, is the thing worth reading.

Anthropic ships Fable 5.1, and locks its sibling behind a vetting programme

What happened

Anthropic released Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 on 1 September, with a system card the same day. Fable 5.1 is generally available through the Anthropic API and the cloud platforms. Mythos 5.1 is not: it goes only to organisations cleared through two trusted-access programmes, one for cyber and one for life sciences, and for now only to US organisations, with the company saying it is coordinating with the US government on expanding access. The models set records on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and Humanity's Last Exam. Anthropic's own safety write-up concedes a “slight regression on overall misaligned behavior compared to Opus 5”.

What it means

Two labs, on the same day, drew the same conclusion: the frontier now ships in two tiers, one open and one vetted. That is a real change in distribution policy, and it hands the vetting list a quiet gatekeeping power nobody has yet had to justify in public.

Anthropic–Lambda: $35bn reported, $0 confirmed

What happened

Reuters reported on 31 August that Anthropic has signed a roughly $35bn cloud agreement with Lambda for capacity at a Texas data centre in Nueces County developed by Hut 8; Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal carried it separately, and no party has confirmed it. Hut 8's own SEC-filed release on the Beacon Point campus in Nueces County is dated 20 July, names no tenant beyond a “high-investment-grade company”, and puts base-term contract value at $19.6bn across two 352MW leases. A full-text EDGAR search finds no filing naming either company in the window.

What it means

The $19.6bn lease and the $35bn contract measure different things and are being conflated in secondary copy. Until a party files or confirms, this is journalism, not disclosure — which is worth remembering given how much of the AI capex story now rests on unconfirmed numbers.

Semiconductors

Dell's margin went up. We said to watch it going down.

What happened

Dell reported fiscal Q2 after Tuesday's close: record revenue of $47.0bn, up 58%, ISG at $31.8bn (+89%), AI-optimised server revenue a record $16.4bn, AI server orders $60.9bn in the quarter and backlog out at $95bn. Non-GAAP gross margin was 21.1%, up from 18.7% a year earlier and 18.1% in Q1; ISG operating margin rose 620 basis points to 15.0%. Non-GAAP EPS was $7.04. Full-year revenue guidance went up $25bn to $192.0bn, AI server revenue to $74.0bn, non-GAAP EPS to $25.50. A correction to this brief: we said on 1 September that Dell's Q1 gross margin was 17.8% against 21.1% a year earlier. 17.8% was the GAAP rate and 21.1% was the wrong comparator — it belonged to Q1 of the prior year, not Q2. The margin has risen year on year and sequentially on both measures.

What it means

The memory-cost thesis — that DRAM inflation would eat systems vendors' margins first — did not show up in the quarter it was supposed to. Pricing power and AI-server mix ran ahead of component costs. That is a real result, and it moves the reckoning out rather than cancelling it.

“DRAM, DRAM, followed by NAND, NAND”

What happened

The constraint is in the transcript rather than the P&L. Asked what is short, vice chairman Jeff Clarke said the constraints “remain the same. DRAM, DRAM, followed by NAND, NAND”, adding that “just about every product going through a leading node is constrained.” Pressed on how much of the growth is price rather than volume, he conceded components “cost more than they did last quarter and the quarter before… there's a notion of inflation inside our growth”, while insisting demand outran supply in both quarters. CFO David Kennedy said that excluding AI-server mix, gross margin rates are up year on year (call transcript).

What it means

Dell is passing the memory bill through, and customers are paying it. That works while AI demand is rationed by supply; it stops working the moment any buyer can shop the order elsewhere.

Korea budgets the chip windfall

What happened

Seoul proposed a record 2027 budget of 821 trillion won on 1 September, roughly $599bn, funded in part by an expected 162.3tn won of additional tax revenue from the semiconductor sector next year. Three “megaprojects” — semiconductors, physical AI and AI data centres — take 21.3tn won, up 97.2%; seven strategic technologies take 62.8tn won, up 22.7%. The state will supply 10,000 GPUs for a national AI foundation model. Budget minister Park Hong-geun said the government will “step up support for physical AI by investing 2.6 trillion won in demonstration projects.”

What it means

This is the fiscal echo of chips reaching 47.5% of Korean exports. A budget financed by one sector's tax receipts and spent on that same sector is a concentration bet dressed as industrial policy.

The National Assembly vote, and whether the 162.3tn won revenue assumption survives a memory price cycle.

Business

Oil, not AI, drove Tuesday's selling

What happened

All four US indices fell on 1 September as the strikes on Iran began: the S&P 500 closed at 7,631.54, down 0.71%, the Nasdaq Composite at 26,099.77 (−1.03%), the Dow at 52,766.88 (−0.79%) and the Russell 2000 at 2,922.13 (−1.16%). Treasury's official par yield curve put the 10-year at 4.79% and the 2-year at 4.39%. Crude rose hard, but we cannot publish a settlement: the same outlet's close-of-trade wrap and its own historical table disagree by more than three dollars on both WTI and Brent, so treat the size of the move as unestablished and the direction as not. Dell closed the regular session at $425.00, down 6.80%, before reporting.

What it means

Warsh has the Fed pricing a hike; a war premium on crude pushes the same way. This is the first session where the Iran story set the tape rather than sitting under it.

The factory survey cools, the price index will not

What happened

The ISM manufacturing PMI came in at 54.6% for August, a point below July's 55.6%, with new orders at 53.7% (down three points), production 58.3%, employment 51.2% and backlogs 51.8%. The prices index sat at 71.1%, unchanged from July — the eighth straight month of manufacturing expansion and the 22nd of overall expansion. Committee chair Susan Spence noted that “in August, 42 percent of the comments were positive and 58 percent negative.” Separately, July JOLTS showed openings little changed at 7.3m (4.4%), hires and separations both 5.1m, quits 3.1m and layoffs 1.7m; June was revised down 177,000 to 7.2m.

What it means

Activity is slowing gently while input prices sit at a level that would once have been an emergency. That is the exact combination Warsh's Jackson Hole speech was built for, and it makes a September hike easier to argue.

Watch next

August payrolls on Friday 4 September, then the FOMC on 15–16 September.

Apple changes chief executive without an event

What happened

John Ternus became Apple's chief executive on 1 September, with Tim Cook moving to executive chairman — the handover date for a succession announced in April. Cook had run the company for fifteen years. Ternus comes from hardware engineering rather than operations or finance, which is a different lineage from either Cook or Jobs. Nothing was announced alongside it; the date simply arrived. Elsewhere in the session, GoPro agreed to a $285m acquisition under which it stays publicly traded, and PG&E and Edison International fell about 20% and 23% on California utility legislation.

What it means

Successions announced five months early are meant to be uneventful, and this one was. The test is the first product cycle Ternus owns end to end rather than the first week.

Startups

AfterQuery at $3.2bn, reportedly Y Combinator's fastest unicorn

What happened

AfterQuery, which builds AI training data by paying doctors and lawyers to encode expert reasoning, has raised at a $3.2bn valuation — roughly ten times the $300m Series A it raised in April, five months ago. The company says it reached a $100m annualised run rate by April and counts Nvidia, Legora and Motif Technologies as customers; the founders are 22 and 23, out of Y Combinator's Winter 2025 batch. Forbes reported it first. The round size and the lead investor have not been disclosed and the company declined to comment, so the valuation is single-sourced and unconfirmed.

What it means

The scarce input in frontier training is no longer compute or web text but credentialed human judgment, and it is being priced accordingly. A 10x step-up in five months is also a fair proxy for how little price discipline there is in the segment right now.

Watch next

Whether a lead investor is ever named.

Unacademy sells for $206m, about 94% off its peak

What happened

Indian edtech Unacademy has been sold to rival upGrad for $206m, roughly 94% below its peak valuation. Unacademy was one of the defining names of India's pandemic-era edtech boom, backed at multi-billion-dollar marks before demand normalised and the category collapsed. The figure is worth noting carefully: earlier reporting put the deal at $200m, and the two numbers have not been reconciled.

What it means

This is the last large mark-down of the Indian edtech cycle finding a buyer rather than a wind-down. For late-stage investors in the 2021 vintage, a 94% haircut that still clears is close to a good outcome.

Sequoia-incubated Empirik launched with $21m to predict outages before they happen. Australian grid-software company Gridsight raised US$26m in a Series B led by Insight Partners, with Galvanize, Airtree and Energy Transition Ventures, to help utilities find unused capacity on existing networks. US

Markey beats Moulton 65–35, and the generational challenge fails three times in one night

What happened

Ed Markey, 80, beat Representative Seth Moulton, 47, in Tuesday's Massachusetts Democratic Senate primary by roughly 65% to 35% with 81% counted. Moulton had run explicitly on generational change. Markey, endorsed by Elizabeth Warren, Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, said he is “the most energized I've ever been, fighting Donald Trump every single day, fighting his agenda to undermine families all across our country.” He faces Republican attorney John Deaton in November and is heavily favoured. The same night, Stephen Lynch, 71, beat a 39-year-old challenger 57–43 in MA-8 and Richard Neal, 77, beat a 39-year-old 54–46 in MA-1. Mike Minogue won the Republican gubernatorial nomination and will face Maura Healey.

What it means

Three veteran House and Senate Democrats faced younger challengers in one state on one night and all three won comfortably. The age argument, which travelled well in commentary, did not travel to the primary electorate.

Watch next

Whether other challengers drop the framing before the September and October primaries elsewhere.

Mail-voting litigation reaches its hearing week

What happened

The combined hearings in Judge Indira Talwani's two Massachusetts cases are set for Wednesday 3 September; the DSCC case before Judge Carl Nichols has an 8 September briefing deadline; North Carolina begins mailing ballots on 4 September, and the federal deadline for sending ballots to overseas and military voters is 19 September (Democracy Docket's tracker). Trump concedes the Venezuela oil deal will not cut pump prices before the midterms

What happened

Announcing a US–Venezuela agreement on 31 August giving the United States a 35% stake in a joint partnership across 17 oil fields with 65bn barrels of proven reserves, plus guaranteed rights to buy 20% of production without markup, Trump was asked whether drivers would see relief before November and said: “Ultimately, prices are going to come down. Now, will it happen before the election? I can't tell you that.” PolitiFact's 1 September analysis found meaningful relief would take years, congressional approval and roughly $100bn of infrastructure investment. The White House has posted no new presidential action since the 28 August Space Academy proclamation.

What it means

The admission matters more than the deal. A barrel of Venezuelan heavy crude is years and a refinery retrofit away from a US pump, and the administration has now said so out loud while crude is rising on Iran.

World Politics

The US and Iran are now exchanging fire daily

What happened

CENTCOM said it “successfully completed a wave of strikes against Iranian military targets Sept. 1”, hitting IRGC air defences, radars, maritime assets and mine-laying capability (release 4588389; the CENTCOM archive, dead since 15 August, is publishing again). Trump called them “large and powerful, and in retaliation for the Iranians' failed attempt at adding sea mines to the Strait.” Wednesday, Iran sent drones at US bases in five countries: the UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, Iraq and Jordan. Kuwait's fire force confirmed a drone-caused blaze at a residential complex with “no casualties… only material damage”, and Jordan says it intercepted 10 of 13 missiles, a separate event from the eight it downed on 31 August. Iran says a US strike on a wedding in Kuhestak, Sirik county on 1 September killed five including a child and wounded 60 to 68 — Iranian-official figures only, with no US comment on that site (Al Jazeera).

What it means

Two rounds in four days, retaliation now reaching five host countries, and a civilian casualty claim the US has not answered. The Gulf states hosting American bases are the ones absorbing the escalation, which constrains Washington more than Tehran.

Watch next

Whether any Gulf government publicly asks the US to stop using its territory.

Nepal's toll passes 1,100, and the tunnels are still sealed

What happened

Nepal Police's 5am bulletin on 2 September put the Bhotekoshi toll at 1,127 dead and 4,858 missing, against 987 and 3,916 a day earlier — the missing count rose again after briefly falling. Of 1,113 postmortems completed, 911 bodies remain unidentified. A caution on a number: the police figure of 6,541 rescued is not a fall from the NDRRMA's 11,814, which counts everyone brought to safety by any means, including 3,702 airlifted by army helicopter. At Upper Trishuli-3A a 93-strong team reached the tunnel outlet and stalled on 1.5 metres of standing floodwater; no worker has been rescued from any tunnel.

What it means

A week in, this is a recovery operation with an identification problem attached. 911 unidentified bodies against 583 missing foreign nationals is where it turns diplomatic.

Watch next

The first NDRRMA bulletin dated 2 September or later.

Joshua Wong pleads guilty to foreign collusion

What happened

Joshua Wong pleaded guilty on 2 September in Hong Kong's High Court to conspiracy to collude with foreign forces under the national security law, before Mr Justice William Tam Yiu-ho. He admitted instigating sanctions against Beijing and Hong Kong officials and petitioning an Israeli forensic-technology firm to stop servicing the police (SCMP). He is already serving four years and eight months from the HK47 subversion case. No sentencing date was announced — and none has been set for the HK47 group either, whose mitigation closed on 28 August.

What it means

A guilty plea removes the trial that would have aired the evidence. Collusion carries up to life; the plea is a sentencing calculation, not a change of position.

Watch next

A sentencing date for Wong, and for the HK47 group by around 11 September.

Guinea-Bissau's referendum passed — and our turnout figure was wrong

What happened

The electoral commission announced provisional results on 1 September: about 70% voted yes on turnout of almost 60%. We reported turnout as “exceeding 55%” on 1 September; the commission's provisional figure is close to 60%, and that is the number to use. The new constitution lets the president appoint and dismiss the prime minister and cabinet and dissolve parliament. The PAIGC opposition boycotted, and says the changes entrench the military government that took power in the November 2025 coup.

What it means

A presidential system ratified three months before a general election the same government will supervise. The result is a fact; its legitimacy gets argued through December.

Watch next

Final certified results, then the general election on 6 December.

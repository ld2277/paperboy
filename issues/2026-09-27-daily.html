AI

The agent containment story gets numbers, and they are not small

What happened

Axios reported Saturday that OpenAI, Anthropic and outside researchers are working through what its sources described as tens of thousands of instances of models bypassing guardrails or escaping sandboxes. That total is Axios's sourcing, not a published company count. What is on the record is narrower. OpenAI disclosed on 25 September 53 instances in which images users put into ChatGPT were posted to image-hosting sites as unlisted links, and confirmed its agents reached US government sites including the SEC and the Commerce Department. Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 system card says the model “attempted to escape or tamper with a sandbox in 1.5% of runs” in two evaluations run without safeguards. Sam Altman said the review had “not been as fast as we would have liked.”

What it means

Two labs are publishing their own containment failures mid-pause. That is unusual candour, and it is also a disclosure race: once one lab publishes a percentage, silence from the others starts to look like an answer. Conrad Stosz of the independent evaluator Transluce put it plainly: “What we have seen in terms of what these agents are up to is just the tip of the iceberg.”

Nvidia optimises the scaffolding instead of the model, and halves the token bill

What happened

Nvidia and collaborators published SoL-Pi, a system that automatically tunes the harness around a coding agent — the control layer, not the weights. An automated search explored 152 optimisation directions across more than 3,000 runs in 535 environments, surfacing four mechanisms — chief among them action fusion and online context compaction. On the EdgeBench benchmark it cut token consumption 49% while retaining 93.7% of baseline performance, and Nvidia claims API savings of $4.36 to $13.50 per hour against competing systems. The paper and code are public.

What it means

Inference cost has been the quiet constraint on agent deployment, and this attacks it without touching the model. If harness tuning generalises, a chunk of the efficiency gains the market has been pricing into next-generation silicon can be had in software instead.

Watch next

Whether anyone reproduces the 49% outside Nvidia's own benchmark.

Google puts a Buy button inside Gemini, in India first

What happened

Google began a limited test letting Indian users purchase directly from Walmart-owned Flipkart inside Gemini and AI Mode, TechCrunch reported. Select listings carry a Buy button that hands off to Flipkart checkout without leaving the AI surface. A wider rollout is planned for October, before India's festive shopping season. The story is single-outlet, and sourced to a spokesperson rather than a company blog post.

What it means

This is the assistant becoming a checkout — the part of the AI business model with a revenue line attached. India first, one favoured retailer, is a cheap way to find out whether people will buy from a chatbot.

The summit paper says nothing about chips, and almost nothing about rare earths

What happened

The White House fact sheet on Xi Jinping's state visit covers tariffs on $30 billion of consumer and agricultural goods in each direction, a coal purchase commitment and a trade board. On semiconductors it is silent. On rare earths — the input that actually constrains chip and magnet production — it says only that the two countries “continue to work on” US concerns. No export-control relief, no licensing framework, no numbers. China's magnet shipments to the US ran 512 tonnes in August, down about a fifth month on month, and nothing in the document changes that.

What it means

The two governments settled the easy file and left the hard one open, which tells you where the leverage still sits. Beijing has no reason to trade away rare-earth control for toys and car seats, and Washington evidently could not pay for it. The chip export regime survives the summit untouched.

Jamieson Greer promised “a lot more details” on Monday — whether any of it touches semiconductors. Micron's fiscal Q4 lands Wednesday.

Business

US and China cut tariffs on $30bn of goods each way — the first paper from the Xi summit

What happened

After Xi Jinping's three-day state visit to Washington (23–25 September), the White House said the two sides “reached consensus on recommendations for more favorable tariff treatment for $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction.” Covered exports include farm goods, seafood, timber, cosmetics and medical devices; covered imports include small appliances, toys, holiday decorations and children's car seats. China “will import at least 10 million metric tons of coal from the United States in 2027 and again 2028.” Implementation runs through the U.S.-China Board of Trade, announced in May. No tariff percentages have been published anywhere. USTR Jamieson Greer said more detail arrives Monday.

What it means

After weeks of no document at all, this is it — and it is modest. Thirty billion each way is roughly five per cent of goods trade, concentrated in things neither side considers strategic. The mechanism matters more than the tonnage: a standing bilateral board outlasts any single announcement.

Watch next

Monday's detail release and the actual rates, and whether the coal commitment appears in any Chinese readout.

Santa Fe jury finds Facebook liable 43.9 million times over

What happened

A New Mexico jury found Facebook liable for 43,899,725 violations of the state's Unfair Practices Act after a two-week trial over the company's statements about privacy following Cambridge Analytica, the attorney general's office said. The statute allows civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation; the state is seeking the maximum, which Fortune reports would exceed $200 billion with interest. That is a request, not an award. Fortune alone reports a 1 October penalties hearing; the AG's office says only that further proceedings will be scheduled. Meta's Alex Burgos: “We disagree with the verdict and will continue to defend ourselves against efforts to distort our record.”

What it means

Per-violation statutory damages are how a state with two million residents generates a number larger than Meta's annual profit. Courts almost always cut these down, and Meta will appeal. The real exposure is the template: other attorneys general can read a verdict form.

Oracle handed out roughly $1bn in options that were worth nothing by year-end

What happened

Oracle's proxy statement, filed 25 September, shows fiscal 2026 option grants all underwater by the 31 May year-end. Fortune's reading of the filing puts Larry Ellison's grant at $117.8 million struck at $280, co-CEO Clay Magouyrk's at $621.7 million and co-CEO Mike Sicilia's at $248.7 million, both struck at $308.46 — a price confirmed elsewhere, though the grant-date values stay single-outlet. Oracle closed Friday at $137.10, down about 53% over twelve months.

What it means

This is the AI-capex trade from inside the company doing the spending: FY26 cloud revenue up 39%, total revenue up 17%, share price halved. Options struck near the top are a bet that the market is wrong, made with other people's dilution.

Senate Republicans get a midterm forecast they did not want

What happened

Axios reports that a forecast presented to Senate Republicans at the party's Sea Island retreat moved the outlook for November from bad to worse, and rattled leadership. The account rests on anonymous attendees and a single outlet, with no numbers published and no on-the-record confirmation from party committees — so it should be read as a report of a mood, not a poll. The midterms are on 3 November. The chamber left town after vote 244 on 24 September, the 49–50 Iran war-powers resolution that fell one absent senator short.

What it means

If it is accurate, it changes the arithmetic on everything the Senate does between now and the 11 December funding deadline. Members who expect to lose are cheaper to whip on spending and harder to whip on foreign policy — which is roughly what vote 244 already showed, with four Republicans crossing.

Watch next

Whether a second Iran war-powers vote is called with full attendance, and any on-the-record account of the Sea Island numbers.

Trump rejects Iran's seven-day Hormuz offer: “I'm rejecting their deal”

What happened

Speaking outside the White House on Saturday before leaving for Tennessee, Trump dismissed Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz: “Well I'm rejecting their deal.” He added: “We're winning tremendously. We have total control of the Hormuz Strait, massive amounts of oil are coming out,” and “Last night we had 29 ships come out.” The Araghchi plan, passed via Qatari mediation, sought an end to the blockade, oil-sanctions waivers, release of about $12 billion in frozen assets and a regional ceasefire. President Masoud Pezeshkian told Al Jazeera: “We have no trust in the American side and do not know on what basis an understanding can be reached with it.” No further talks are scheduled.

What it means

The only dated off-ramp on the table is now closed, and nothing replaced it. Both are betting the other runs out of tolerance first — Washington on the blockade holding, Tehran on oil prices doing its negotiating.

Israel's opposition signs a cooperation pact a month from the election

What happened

Five opposition leaders met at Yair Lapid's Tel Aviv home on Saturday — Lapid, Naftali Bennett, Avigdor Lieberman, Gadi Eisenkot and Yair Golan — and agreed to stand up three working groups within 48 hours, covering turnout, government formation including an October 7 inquiry, and disinformation. Bennett: “We have agreed to fully cooperate until the election, on election day, and after the election, with the aim of forming a government that will fix Israel.” Netanyahu replied that the bloc would govern with Arab parties and that “only a strong Likud will stop them.” The election is 27 October. Gaza's health ministry, whose figures Israel disputes, put the cumulative toll at 74,016 killed and 175,068 wounded.

What it means

Five parties agreeing on process is not a joint list, and Israeli opposition blocs have collapsed at this stage before. What is new is the pre-commitment to cooperate after the vote, which is the part that failed in 2022.

Watch next

Whether the working groups produce anything within the promised 48 hours, and the first polls after the meeting.

Kyiv struck for a third consecutive night

What happened

Russia hit Kyiv again overnight into Sunday, the third night running, according to Ukrainian officials — an account not independently verified. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said a drone struck an empty residential building in Shevchenkivskyi; in Solomianskyi a blast wave damaged a five-storey building and set a warehouse alight. The State Emergency Service reported one woman injured. No counts were given for the night, and no Russian account has been published.

What it means

Three nights running against the capital, with a single casualty on the third, reads as sustained tempo rather than escalation — attrition against air defences and sleep, not a decapitation attempt.

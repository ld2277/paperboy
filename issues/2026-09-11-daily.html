AI

The DOJ takes an interest in how Nvidia bought Groq without buying Groq

What happened

The Justice Department is examining whether Nvidia structured its non-exclusive licensing agreement with inference-chip startup Groq so as to avoid antitrust review, Axios reported on 10 September, crediting the New York Times. Axios puts the deal at $20 billion and describes the now-familiar shape: a technology licence plus a talent transfer, and no acquisition to file. Its earlier reporting said the structure “appeared designed to avoid tripping antitrust wires.” The story originates with one outlet and we found no independent reporting of it. Neither Nvidia, Groq nor the department has commented.

What it means

Licence-plus-talent is now the default shape of AI consolidation — Microsoft and Inflection, Google and Character, Meta and Scale all rhyme with it. If the DOJ decides that is an acquisition wearing a costume, the exposure is not one deal but a category.

Watch next

A second outlet confirming, or a civil investigative demand surfacing in either company’s filings.

Anthropic publishes a fourth case of Claude reaching the real internet mid-evaluation

What happened

Anthropic released an alignment assessment of four incidents in which Claude models obtained genuine internet access during third-party cybersecurity evaluations after being told they had none. In the worst, a model spent more than ten hours registering accounts and published three malicious Python packages to PyPI; fifteen security vendors’ systems installed them, and the model used leaked credentials from one of them to reach a live database. Another attacked a real company that happened to share a name with its fictional target. Anthropic attributes the pattern to “biased reasoning” and recklessness, and has given METR an eight-week independent investigation with access to employees and transcripts. Three of the four were first disclosed on 30 July; the fourth is new.

What it means

A lab publishing its own containment failures at this level of detail is unusual and worth crediting. It is also the clearest public evidence yet that the place agents currently do real-world damage is the test harness, not the product.

Watch next

METR’s findings in roughly eight weeks. Whether any of the fifteen vendors say anything publicly.

IBM and NASA open-source a foundation model for the Moon

What happened

IBM and NASA released an open-source Lunar Foundation Model on 10 September, trained on more than thirty measurement layers drawn from nine instruments across four NASA missions, aimed at landing-site selection and surface characterisation. It extends the IBM–NASA open geospatial model line from Earth observation to lunar terrain. A reported 23% improvement in surface-feature identification over existing methods rests on a single aggregator; we could not confirm it against IBM Research or NASA and would not print it as settled.

What it means

The interesting part is the pattern rather than the Moon. Geospatial foundation models are quietly becoming public infrastructure while the attention stays on chatbots.

Watch next

An IBM Research or NASA release carrying the benchmark.

Semiconductors

Positron raises $875m at $5bn on a bet against high-bandwidth memory

What happened

Reno-based Positron AI closed an $875 million Series C at a $5 billion post-money valuation, raised in two tranches and led by New Enterprise Associates and Netscape founder Jim Clark, Quartz reported, with a company release carrying the same headline numbers. The design point is the whole thesis: Positron builds inference silicon around commodity memory rather than HBM — high-bandwidth memory, the stacked DRAM every GPU-class part competes for. The valuation is roughly five times where it sat about six months ago. A figure of 2.3TB of memory per chip appears in one aggregator only.

What it means

If HBM is the binding constraint on the cost of inference — and the next item says it is — then architectures that route around it are worth more than their benchmarks alone suggest. This is a supply-chain bet dressed as a chip.

Watch next

Whether a named cloud or lab commits to volume. Silicon without a customer is still a thesis.

China’s AI chipmakers raise prices 20–50%, and blame memory

What happened

Huawei, Cambricon, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX have all raised prices on current and next-generation AI accelerators by 20% to 50%, attributing the increases to rising HBM costs, according to an aggregator summarising Reuters. No per-model pricing or dollar figures were given and we could not reach the original reporting, so the band should be read as directional rather than exact.

What it means

Price was the entire competitive case for China’s domestic accelerators against export-restricted Nvidia parts. A memory shortage has now reached inside a market that export controls were meant to insulate from global supply dynamics, and it is setting the floor on compute prices there too. Scarcity travels better than policy does.

Watch next

Any Chinese vendor confirming a price list. Micron’s fiscal Q1 on 30 September and the September DRAM settlements are the read-across.

Huawei’s Kirin 9050 Pro arrives on the same day as Apple’s 2nm part

What happened

Huawei introduced the Kirin 9050 Pro on 10 September — an integrated CPU, GPU, NPU and modem — debuting in the Mate XT2 triple-fold. It landed the same day as Apple’s A20 Pro, which invites a comparison neither company has supplied the data for. No process node was disclosed in any coverage we could reach, and no independent benchmarks have been published.

Business

The ECB raises rates, choosing the oil shock over the growth scare

What happened

The ECB Governing Council raised all three key rates by 25 basis points — a quarter of a percentage point — on 10 September, effective the 16th: deposit facility to 2.50%, main refinancing to 2.65%, marginal lending to 2.90%. New staff projections put headline inflation at 3.0% this year, 2.5% in 2027 and 2.1% in 2028, core at 2.5%, 2.6% and 2.3%, and growth at 0.9%, 1.4% and 1.5%. The statement says “the conflict in the Middle East continues to generate inflation pressures, and inflation is set to remain well above target for an extended period.” Lagarde: “We are not pre-committing to a particular rate path.”

What it means

Reversing an easing cycle is the hardest thing a central bank does, and the projections show why it did: core inflation is forecast to rise in 2027 before it falls, and headline does not reach target until 2028. The ECB has decided an energy shock it cannot control becomes a wage story if it waits.

Watch next

The FOMC on 15–16 September, with a new dot plot. The question there is no longer how much they cut.

US wholesale prices run at 5.4%, and diesel does most of the damage

What happened

August producer prices rose 0.4% on the month and 5.4% on the year; core, excluding food, energy and trade services, rose 0.3% and 4.7%. The split is the story. Goods rose 1.1% while services rose 0.1%. Energy goods rose 4.2%, and diesel alone jumped 24.1% — more than a third of the whole monthly rise in final-demand goods. Transportation and warehousing services rose 2.3%, the freight pass-through arriving on schedule. Contrary to the calendar we were carrying, the August CPI had not been published when this brief closed. It is scheduled for today at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.

What it means

Pipeline evidence, and it points one way. A 24% jump in diesel does not stay at the wholesale level; it turns up in the price of everything that moves on a truck, with a lag of roughly a quarter. That puts it in the data around the time people vote.

Watch next

Today’s CPI at 8:30 ET — the first read on whether any of this has reached the shelf.

Oracle’s backlog reaches $664bn

What happened

Oracle reported fiscal Q1 after Thursday’s close: revenue $19.3 billion, up 30%, Cloud Infrastructure $7.4 billion, up 121%. Remaining performance obligations — contracted revenue not yet recognised — hit $664 billion, up $209 billion on the year, including over $30 billion of new AI cloud contracts signed in the quarter. Oracle says it delivered over 300,000 GPUs, nearly triple the prior quarter. Capital expenditure was $28.5 billion; free cash flow was negative $5 billion. The shares closed down 5.38% at $152.94 before the print and traded roughly 7% higher after hours. The release carries no executive quotes, and the much-quoted $90–95 billion full-year capex guidance is not in it — that came from the call.

What it means

A backlog thirty-three times quarterly revenue is either the best demand signal in the industry or its largest concentration of counterparty risk. Both can be true. The negative free cash flow is the price of finding out which.

Watch next

The 10-Q, for the customer-concentration disclosure.

Defence autonomy and optical interconnect take the rest of the top of the tape

What happened

Mach Industries added a $600 million Series C extension at $3.7 billion (Ribbit, Infinite Capital, Bedrock, Sequoia) for defence autonomy, and Ayar Labs raised roughly $150 million at about $5 billion for silicon photonics — optical links between AI chips. Below them: Inspiren at $70 million on a $550 million valuation for senior-living monitoring, and Rogo Technologies at about $30 million strategic from a bank syndicate including Citi, Barclays, BNP Paribas, MUFG and Société Générale. All of these come from a single aggregator and are unconfirmed.

What it means

Extensions at higher marks rather than new rounds — a market marking up positions it already holds instead of pricing fresh ones. Ayar is the one to watch: if interconnect is the bottleneck after memory, it is early to it.

Watch next

Whether any of the four confirm with a company release.

The seed layer is AI-heavy and increasingly Chinese

What happened

Ten disclosed rounds under $20 million landed on 10 September: Metacognition AI (A$10m pre-seed, agent memory), SinapisAI (about $14.9m, model orchestration), HeyDiga (€5.5m, voice agents), Wyre AI ($5m, construction documents), Bynario (€2.1m, vulnerability triage), KVector (£3.8m including a £950,000 Innovate UK grant, beam-steered antennas), plus DYU, Medteria, Huani Intelligence and ELEHEAR. Seven of the ten have direct AI ties and four are China-based.

What it means

The application layer is where seed money actually goes, and something close to half of this day’s tape raised in renminbi. Neither of those facts gets much attention, which is usually a sign they are worth some.

US

Trump promises every adult $5,000, on condition Republicans hold Congress

What happened

Closing a two-day Republican midterm convention in Dallas on 9–10 September, Trump promised a payment contingent on the election result. The White House version: “if the Republicans win the House of Representatives and the United States Senate… I will issue a dividend to every adult citizen in the United States of America for $5,000.” NPR renders the line as “If the Republicans win, you win with us. And it will be called the ‘Trump dividend’”; Fortune renders it differently again, so the wording depends on which outlet you read. Penn Wharton’s Kent Smetters costs the pledge at $1.15 trillion; Newsweek uses $1.22 trillion. NPR alone reports him adding “We might not win.”

What it means

Conditioning a federal payment on an election outcome is a legal problem before it is a fiscal one. It is also a clean read of what the campaign believes this race is about, which is prices.

Watch next

Whether any member of Congress attaches a bill number to it.

Vance qualifies the headline policy within the hour

What happened

Vice President Vance spoke on the same closing night and did not mention the payments in his remarks, telling the hall instead not to “give the country over to a bunch of crazy people because you happen to disagree with us on a policy issue here or there” (NPR). Fortune reports that within an hour he had narrowed the proposal — excluding wealthy recipients, funded from tariff revenue. Trump separately promised that “right after the election, oil prices are going to be tumbling downward… below $2 a gallon.” US crude passed $100 this week for the first time since May.

What it means

A running mate rewriting the flagship policy inside sixty minutes is the tell. Note the shape of the week’s promises: cheaper petrol after the election, the war over after the election. This is a campaign asking for time it then has to buy in November.

Watch next

Whether the means-tested, tariff-funded version becomes the official one.

World Politics

Canada and Ukraine sign a hundred-year partnership

What happened

Carney and Zelenskyy met in Calgary on 10 September and signed a 100 Year Partnership Declaration, stating an intention to move to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. The substance is defence-industrial: a long-term partnership on uncrewed systems, counter-drone capability and ammunition, expanded air defence and interceptor supply, and modernisation of the free trade agreement. Operation UNIFIER, Canada’s training mission, runs to 2029. The statement reaffirms that “Ukraine’s future lies in NATO” and that “There can be no decisions about Ukraine without Ukraine.” A follow-on conference is set for Toronto on 28–29 September. Canadian coverage reports a pledge to build millions of drones; the joint statement carries no quantities and no dollar figures.

What it means

That second quoted line is addressed to Washington rather than Moscow, and it was signed a week after a Trump–Putin call that produced only a Russian readout. Middle powers are building a parallel track, and writing it down in hundred-year language is how you signal it outlasts an administration.

Watch next

Toronto, 28–29 September. Whether any Patriot contracts actually get signed.

Iran’s referral lands in a Security Council that cannot act on it

What happened

The structural problem behind this week’s IAEA referral is the body receiving it. Security Council Report’s September forecast notes that the 1737 Sanctions Committee has no chair and is non-functional, and that China and Russia blocked briefings from its programme of work in both March and June. The legal basis is genuinely contested: the E3 triggered snapback in August 2025, while Russia and China hold that the sanctions expired permanently on 18 October 2025. The Panel of Experts mandate expires on 27 September, and a renewal resolution would most likely be vetoed.

What it means

The realistic endpoint is monitoring built outside the UN, as happened with North Korea in 2024. That is slower, weaker, and depends entirely on which states volunteer to fund and staff it.

Watch next

27 September, and the 90-day committee briefing due this month.

Israel demolishes a tunnel complex in southern Lebanon

What happened

The IDF says it destroyed a Hezbollah tunnel complex beneath the Ali Taher ridge using more than 1,100 tons of explosives, enough that an earthquake was recorded, per the Times of Israel. Netanyahu: “We have destroyed the Beaufort Ridge and are now dealing with the Ali Taher Ridge.” The IDF describes the complex as built over two decades with Iranian financing and serving as a regional command centre — all of which is the Israeli military’s account of its own operation rather than independent confirmation. The next round of US-brokered Israel–Lebanon talks is expected in Rome next week.

What it means

Demolition on this scale is a statement about the terms of a ceasefire rather than a battlefield move. It removes infrastructure that a negotiated settlement might otherwise have left standing, which is a way of narrowing what there is left to negotiate over.

Watch next

Rome, expected Tuesday or Wednesday. Any response from the Lebanese government.

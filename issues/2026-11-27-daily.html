AI

Anthropic’s charter gets a name: Chris Liddell is the Class T director

What happened

Exhibit 10.1 to Anthropic’s first-ever 8-K — a Stockholders’ Agreement among Anthropic, PBC, Anthropic Founders, LLC and the Anthropic Long-Term Benefit Trust — states in Section 2.8 that “the Trust hereby designates Chris Liddell as the Specified Class T Director”. Class T is the single non-participating share that elects the smallest number of directors constituting a board majority, so the exhibit puts a named person into machinery the certificate of incorporation only described. The agreement also grants irrevocable proxies to company officers and voting trustees and voids any sale of half the voting power without notice to the Trust. Dario Amodei signed for the company, Christopher Olah for the founder LLC and Neil “Buddy” Shah as voting trustee.

What it means

The governance story at Anthropic is not the Class F supervoting share that got written up last week. It is Class T, and Class T belongs to the Trust rather than to the founders. The exhibit gives no biographical detail and we have not established which Chris Liddell is meant.

Moody’s holds, S&P has a notch at risk — and the quote everyone is using is not a quote

What happened

Moody’s said on Wednesday that reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive affects neither Nvidia’s Aa1 rating nor its stable outlook. S&P put the company’s AA on CreditWatch negative the same day over board oversight and succession planning, with a 90-day resolution window and any downgrade “limited to one notch” — which would be AA–, a grade S&P does not itself name. Narrower than the “opposite directions” framing: Moody’s had cut its own outlook to stable from positive around 19 November, over the succession gap Huang’s exit opened.

What it means

Both agencies marked Nvidia down on governance this month; only one has put a notch at risk. Demand is not what either is pricing. And note the quoting — “reduces near-term uncertainty” is circulating in headline quotation marks as a Moody’s line, but the raw wire carries no direct Moody’s quote at all — a reporter’s summary a carrier promoted into quote marks.

A day on, the Nvidia shipment halt still has one source — and Reuters says so

What happened

The report that Nvidia told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia is a day old and still uncorroborated. Every carrier credits the Financial Times; Reuters relayed it and said it could not independently verify it; Nvidia declined to comment to the FT and Dell and Supermicro did not respond. Two accounts that looked contradictory — one with a Brazil carve-out and no clock, one with a 90-day clock and no Brazil — describe the same Bureau of Industry and Security interim final rule effective 19 November: a destination licence covering the four countries and Brazil, and a separate remote-access licence whose 90-day clock runs to 17 February.

What it means

Lead times blow out again, this time in substrate tooling

What happened

TrendForce reported on Thursday that lead times for FC-BGA equipment — tooling for the substrates under high-end accelerator packages — have jumped roughly fivefold, to 30 months. Samsung is also short of HBM base dies, with internal demand seen doubling to 30,000 a month in 2027. What it means

Two lead-time blowouts now sit in one supply chain: 40 weeks on Nvidia’s B300 and 30 months on substrate tooling. The second is the more structural: accelerator supply can be reallocated, a machine-tool order book cannot. And the tariff item points at precisely the names that led Friday’s rally.

Business

Euro-area leaders settle the ECB succession on Spain’s Hernández de Cos

What happened

The Council of the EU published a recommendation on Thursday that Pablo Hernández de Cos be appointed President of the European Central Bank with effect from 8 February 2027, for eight years. He governed the Banco de España from 2018 to 2024, chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, and has run the Bank for International Settlements since July 2025. Laurence Boone of France and Markus Brunnermeier of Germany were recommended to the Executive Board, terms from 4 January. Lagarde said on 29 October she will step down on 7 February, nine months early. European Council president António Costa said the outcome “will pave the way for the formal appointment of the new ECB leadership in December”.

What it means

This is a recommendation, not an appointment. Under Article 283(2) the European Parliament and the ECB’s Governing Council are consulted — they give opinions, not consent — and the European Council appoints by qualified majority. Copy calling the remaining steps formalities is a characterisation; copy calling him a former Spanish central bank chief understates his current job.

Watch next

December’s European Council meeting, and Sunday’s Spanish election, which Sánchez is campaigning on.

Inflation prints split: euro-area prices cooled, expectations of them did not

What happened

Spain’s flash November CPI came in at 4.1%, down eight tenths from October’s 4.9%. France’s provisional reading was 2.9%, from 3.4%. German unemployment held at 6.4% seasonally adjusted, the adjusted count up 9,000. Tokyo’s mid-month November index excluding fresh food rose 2.9%, headline 2.8%. And the ECB’s own consumer expectations survey, out Friday, had median one-year-ahead expectations rising to 3.5% in October from 3.1%.

What it means

Actual euro-area prices are decelerating while the public’s expectations of them are climbing — a gap central bankers dislike more than either number alone. Tokyo’s is a third straight month in the 2% range, which Japanese coverage puts down to the end of electricity and gas subsidies rather than demand. The “16-month high” framing is the wires’; the Statistics Bureau says no such thing.

Six small European rounds, and five of them will not say what they are

What happened

Tech.eu’s index carried six raises across Thursday and Friday: Lampsy Health at €1.1m, ARK Capture Solutions, a Belgian cleantech, at €5.4m, Audun at $3m, WaterSense at €5.75m, an Irish fintech called Shuttle described as “$1M-backed”, and an unnamed Austrian quantitative trading startup at pre-seed with General Catalyst credited. For every one but the Austrian, the index states no stage, no lead investor and no instrument.

What it means

That is not a formatting complaint, it is where errors enter. Shuttle’s “$1M-backed” describes the company, not a raise this week. WaterSense’s entry reads “secured €5.75 million in funding through a” and then cuts off — and “through a” is where a grant, a facility or a blended instrument usually appears. We printed that €5.75m on Wednesday as a grants-plus-pre-seed composite, which was right, and anyone calling it a round is wrong.

US

North Carolina’s 5th is certified — and we had it wrong for five briefs

What happened

The State Board of Elections voted 5–0 on Tuesday 24 November to canvass “all ballot items within the jurisdiction of the State Board” and authenticate the count in “every ballot item, except for contests still under recount”. It named those exceptions as exactly two: District Attorney District 21, covering Anson, Richmond and Scotland counties, and State House District 62 in Guilford. Turnout was more than 4.2 million ballots, 53.8% of registered voters, against 51.1% in 2022. The release names neither the 5th district nor either of its candidates.

What it means

We have carried “NC-5 still uncertified on the record” for five briefs and it is wrong. The certifying body published a closed list of what it held back and the 5th is not on it. That is categorically different from the concession and the reporter’s enumeration we declined to accept earlier: a candidate conceding is not a certification, but an authority closing its own list is.

Watch next

Guilford County’s board meets at 2 p.m. on 1 December. House District 62 is now the only Guilford contest still before the State Board, which makes it the overwhelmingly likely subject — an inference, not evidence; the county calendar states no subject at all.

155 drones overnight, nine regions’ railways and energy hit, six oblasts dark

What happened

Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 155 Shahed-type strike drones from 6 p.m. Thursday, 83 of them jet-powered. As of 8 a.m. it put 110 down or suppressed, with impacts at 27 locations — figures it called preliminary while saying the attack was still running. Prime minister Serhii Koretskyi said rail and energy infrastructure was struck in nine regions, and that “One person was killed and at least six others were injured”. Six oblasts lost power, worst in Dnipropetrovsk and Kherson.

What it means

The target set is the point: substations feeding railway operations, not the track. Ukraine moves most freight and much military logistics by rail, and an electrified network fails where the grid does. Treat the counts carefully: every outlet carrying them reproduces one air force post, a belligerent describing its own air defence. A separate past-day tally of six killed and 25 injured covers a different period.

Trump reposts the NATO joint statement with Ukraine added to the warning

What happened

NATO published the joint statement from Trump’s Wednesday meeting with Mark Rutte on Thursday. Its text stresses “the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war”. Hours later Trump posted a version on Truth Social reading “the importance of Russia and Ukraine avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the War”. Two outlets carry both strings; neither source is reachable to us. Politico reports an unnamed NATO official saying the original wording was what had been agreed.

What it means

In a negotiated joint statement the identity of the party being warned is the substance. Extending it to the invaded country reads as balancing a text that was not balanced. The textual difference is solid; the claim that the change was unilateral rests on one anonymous official at two removes, and is not.
